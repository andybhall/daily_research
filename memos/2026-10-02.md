# Dataset Hunt — 2026-10-02

**Frontier:** 5. Institutional records (hearing transcripts, public comments, agendas, visitor logs, staffing/payroll, calendars)
**Verdict:** The notice-and-comment record is an open, longitudinal panel of who mobilizes to shape U.S. AI governance — and it's the one civic channel generative AI is now visibly corroding.

Fourth pass on frontier 5 (08-13 Federal AI Use Case Inventory + NL/UK registers; 09-02 NCSL bills, Federal Register rulemaking, Congressional hearings; 09-22 GAO oversight). Those are the *systems*, the *rules*, and the *auditor*. The un-mined institutional record — and one literally named in the frontier — is **public comments**: the public's side of the rulemaking, not the government's.

## Top find: public comments on federal AI rulemakings (regulations.gov)

Every federal AI rule and inquiry opens a notice-and-comment docket, and [regulations.gov](https://www.regulations.gov) posts every submission with a documented bulk API. The AI dockets are a byproduct panel of who fights over AI governance — firms, trade groups, artists, agencies — and, increasingly, of bots.

**AI nexus + agenda.** Intrinsic: the comments *are* about governing AI. Bullseye **Governance** (contesting the rules that will bind AI) and **Representation** (notice-and-comment as a participation channel — now distorted by AI-generated mass comments).

**Why it clears the bar.** Revealed positions filed under real stakes by organized interests (a4); comments accrue incidentally as a rulemaking byproduct across 2023→2026 dockets (b4); the *genre* (comments-as-data) is well-mined, but the **AI-specific cross-docket participation panel is unbuilt** — no corpus analysis of who shapes AI rules surfaced (c3); panel-able by docket × commenter × organization × position × date (d4); dead-on agenda with an intrinsic AI nexus (e5). **Score: 20/25** (a4 b4 c3 d4 e5).

**Evidence from a live probe.** I queried the regulations.gov API this run: the **U.S. Copyright Office AI Notice of Inquiry (docket COLC-2023-0006) holds 10,371 comments** (`meta.totalElements`, DEMO_KEY) — the training-data/copyright fight that drew artists and creators en masse ([copyright.gov/ai](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/); 34 questions, 10,000+ responses, some >100 pages). By contrast the technical governance inquiries drew mostly organized interests: the **NIST AI EO RFI** got **214** ([nist.gov](https://www.nist.gov/artificial-intelligence/executive-order-safe-secure-and-trustworthy-artificial-intelligence/request)) and the **OMB draft AI memo M-24-10** got **196** ([whitehouse.gov](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/M-24-10-Advancing-Governance-Innovation-and-Risk-Management-for-Agency-Use-of-Artificial-Intelligence.pdf)). The distortion is now official: a **GAO report (July 21, 2026)** found the IRS flooded with tens of thousands of comments, some AI-generated, and that "IRS has not developed policies for addressing mass public comments or comments written with the assistance of AI"; FCC staff reportedly reviewed AI-generated *summaries* of comments rather than the comments themselves ([Nextgov](https://www.nextgov.com/artificial-intelligence/2024/05/house-bill-targets-ai-generated-comments-rulemaking/396419/); [CFO Brew](https://www.cfobrew.com/stories/irs-turn-to-ai-to-tackle-deluge-of-public-comments)). For scale, the NY AG flagged 18M+ non-authentic comments in the 2017 net-neutrality docket ([GW Reg Studies](https://regulatorystudies.columbian.gwu.edu/will-chatgpt-break-notice-and-comment-regulations)). Artifacts in [`analysis/2026-10-02/`](../analysis/2026-10-02/SUMMARY.md).

**Access & cost.** Free; a keyed regulations.gov API lifts the DEMO_KEY rate limit for a full pull (comment text, organization, date, attachments) across every AI docket. A weekend to build the docket × commenter × position panel and layer an AI-generated-comment classifier on top. **Nearest work / difference:** comments-as-data is established (Balla on mass-comment campaigns; influence-tracing work), but nobody has assembled the cross-docket *AI-governance participation* panel or measured how AI-generated submissions are reshaping it. **Headline figure:** share of comments from industry vs. public vs. likely-AI-generated, by AI docket, 2023→2026.
