# Dataset Hunt — 2026-11-18

**Frontier:** 2. Money trails — procurement, financial disclosures, grants, subawards, contracts
**Verdict:** A global, openly-downloadable panel of government AI-procurement contracts — the cross-national counterpart to the US-only USASpending AI slice.

## Top find: World Bank "AI Procurement Contracts" (dataset 0067129)

A CC-BY Excel the World Bank quietly published in July 2026 as supporting data for the *World Development Report 2026: The Promise of Artificial Intelligence*: the **AI-related subset of ~6.1M government procurement notices** its team classified with an LLM, pulled from three real tender streams — the EU's TED, the World Bank's own contracts, and the Open Contracting Partnership ([catalog](https://datacatalog.worldbank.org/search/dataset/0067129/ai-procurement-contracts); [reproducibility pkg](https://reproducibility.worldbank.org/catalog/624)). Each row is one government's contract to buy an AI system, with the tender title plus classification fields (`task_result`, `tool_category`, `autonomy_result`, income group). This is **procurement exhaust** — revealed spending under budget and political stakes, piling up over two decades — not a survey or a constructed benchmark.

Why it's unclaimed and worth taking: the row-level panel has been used for exactly two aggregate figures (WDR-2026 Fig 5.2/5.7) and nothing else. A real-equivalent Scholar/web sweep this run found **no scholarly cross-country AI-procurement panel** exists — one AI-index report names "the absence of a single global dataset for AI procurement volumes" as a field-wide gap ([Alice Labs](https://alicelabs.ai/reports/global-public-sector-ai-index-2026)). The nearest scholarly object is Fazekas et al.'s Global Public Procurement Dataset (72M contracts, 42 countries, 2006–2021) — **general** procurement with no AI tagging ([livinglib](https://thelivinglib.org/global-contract-level-public-procurement-dataset)). Our object is the AI slice *plus* a what-does-the-AI-do taxonomy the GPPD lacks. (The gemmozero/ai-procurement-2026 HF table that surfaces alongside is the unreliable vendor set — post-dated Palantir/OpenAI rows, no WB link — and is ignored.)

Access is a free 3MB `.xlsx` download + a DDH API ([file](https://datacatalogfiles.worldbank.org/ddh-published/0067129/DR0096198/AI_Procurement_Contracts.xlsx)); collection cost is a weekend, not a quarter. **Headline figure nobody has drawn:** the global composition of state AI buying by function — and in particular how much of it is surveillance/biometric. **First analysis:** do governments with weaker democratic institutions procure disproportionately more Surveillance & Biometric AI? Identification: within-country over time (23-year notice dates) against income group / a democracy index, using the 102 countries with ≥20 contracts.

**Evidence from a live sample:** I pulled the full file this run and parsed it with a stdlib xlsx reader. **17,685 AI contracts, notice dates 2003→2025**; sources Open Contracting 10,211 / EU TED 4,866 / World Bank 2,608; **1,342 country/region codes, 102 countries with ≥20 contracts** (max 1,665 = Netherlands). Income group: High 14,428 (82%), Upper-mid 1,437, Lower-mid 1,391, Low 429. Top functions: Predictive Analytics 5,512, Remote Sensing 2,099, Environmental Monitoring 1,604, Speech/Document 1,236, **Surveillance & Biometric 1,159**, AI Infrastructure 1,081. One fact you can't get from the landing page: the released `model` field is **`deepseek` for 100% of rows**, though the catalog page credits GPT-5 — a provenance flag worth carrying. Artifact: [`analysis/2026-11-18/`](https://github.com/andybhall/daily_research/tree/main/analysis/2026-11-18).

## Shortlist
- **Campaign payments to AI vendors (FEC, "39 candidates paid OpenAI")** | ~16 | Real Schedule-B exhaust, but WaPo/CNBC/Fast Company already built it this cycle on maximally-mined FEC data — fails unclaimed ([aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/wapo-39-campaigns-paid-openai-despite-its-political-ad-ban)).
- **SBIR/STTR AI awards (sbir.gov open API, 220k+ awards)** | ~14 | Open and longitudinal, but AI isn't labeled (keyword-only), the source is heavily mined, and R&D funding sits off the political agenda (which lists procurement, not research grants) — same profile as the logged NSF-grants reject ([sbir.gov](https://www.sbir.gov/awards)).
- **Data-center ballot-measure finance, 2026 (Ballotpedia, 38–41 local measures)** | ~13 | A genuine 2026 money story, but the AI nexus is indirect (land-use/zoning), finance data is scattered, and Ballotpedia's tracker is curated, not exhaust ([Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Data_center-related_ballot_measures,_2026)).
- **UK Contracts Finder AI spend (Tussell/Resultsense, Palantir = 30% of value)** | ~15 | On-agenda and striking, but figures live behind commercial trackers; no open AI-labeled register — the WB find already ingests TED/UK-style tenders.
- **Section 117 foreign gifts to universities (ED portal)** | ~15 | Aggregate-only, no AI-labeled slice, weak intrinsic AI nexus.
