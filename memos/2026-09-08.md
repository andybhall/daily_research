# Dataset Hunt — 2026-09-08

**Frontier:** 1. Legal / judicial exhaust (dockets, sanctions orders, amicus briefs, discovery exhibits, consent decrees, PACER/RECAP corners)
**Verdict:** A fresh, award-winning-but-barely-reused coded panel of 168 lawsuits over automated decisions — hiring, credit, benefits, policing — with dockets, outcomes, and system types, openly downloadable.

Third pass on frontier 1 (08-09 Opioid archive, 08-19 AI Hallucination Cases). The AI-native legal corners left un-mined: not hallucination or copyright (both in the ledger), but the litigation over **algorithmic decisions made about people** — and biometric/AI class actions.

## Top find: Algorithmic Contestability Cases Database (Stewart, FAccT 2026)

The supplementary dataset for a FAccT 2026 **Best Paper**, "[Beyond Explanation: Evidentiary Rights for Algorithmic Accountability](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22716)" ([ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3805689.3806532)), deposited openly on [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/18340254) (CC-BY, concept DOI [10.5281/zenodo.18069759](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.18069759)). It codes **168 lawsuits, settlements, and regulatory actions where a person contested an automated decision** across employment, credit, criminal justice, benefits, housing, and health.

**AI nexus + agenda.** Bullseye **Governance** — how courts and regulators adjudicate algorithmic decision-making inside institutions. AI nexus is real, not nominal: I computed the `algorithm_type` field — **63% machine-learning, 20% statistical models**, only 14% rule-based.

**Why it clears the bar.** Real litigation under legal stakes, each with a docket (a4); the underlying cases accrue over 2012→2025, though the coding is assembled (b3); **barely reused** — the deposit shows **20 downloads**, and a Scholar/web check finds only the origin paper, no third-party analysis (c3); coded on domain × jurisdiction × year × outcome, supporting within-domain panels (d4); dead-on **Governance** (e5). **Score: 19/25** (a4 b3 c3 d4 e5). Chosen as top over DAIL below because I could fully download and verify it this run.

**Evidence from a live sample.** I pulled the 56 kB deposit and analyzed `cases.csv`: **168 cases, 18 fields**; **165 carry a docket number, 165 a primary-source URL, 157 marked Verified** (e.g. *EEOC v. iTutorGroup*, ML hiring age-bias, $365K, docket 1:22-cv-02565 EDNY). Domain split: Employment 39, Criminal Justice 32, Credit 28, Government Benefits 27, Housing 20, Health 13. Outcomes: **Achieved 102, Ongoing 48, Denied 18**; consequences include 37 policy changes, 32 monetary, 14 systems discontinued. The paper's own headline (from the data): cases **without** evidence access succeed ~**9%**, **with** access ~**97%**. Artifacts in [`analysis/2026-09-08/`](../analysis/2026-09-08/SUMMARY.md).

**Access & cost.** One CC-BY zip, no auth — an afternoon to load and extend. **Nearest work / difference:** the origin paper uses it narrowly to argue for evidentiary rights (access → win-rate); nobody has mined the panel for the obvious questions — which domains and plaintiff types win, how outcomes shift by jurisdiction and year, ML vs. rule-based liability. **Headline figure:** win rate by domain × evidentiary-access level, with liability-shield domains flagged.

## Runner-ups

**DAIL — the Database of AI Litigation** (GW Ethical Tech Initiative; [etidail.com](https://etidail.com/), [GW blog](https://blogs.gwu.edu/law-eti/ai-litigation-database/)). The broadest living tracker of AI litigation — **~260 cases** "from complaint forward," ~40 of them generative-AI copyright, the rest hiring/credit/sentencing/companion-liability. Ongoing and AI-native; distinct from our copyright-only (Mishcon) and hallucination-only (Charlotin) ledger finds. **Could not probe:** the site is a JS app with no visible bulk export/API this run, so no live pull — a scrape is the access path. **Score: 19/25** (a4 b3 c3 d4 e5).

**S.T.O.P. BIPA Litigation Tracker** ([stopspying.org](https://www.stopspying.org/bipa-litigation-tracker)). **182 Illinois biometric-privacy lawsuits**, of which **51 allege facial-geometry/facial-recognition** collection (35 against apps like TikTok, Snapchat, photo services) — the AI-facial-recognition subset is the nexus. Downloadable as Excel + PDF; fields for context, biometric type, court results. Governance, panel-able by company/year. Caveat: BIPA broadly (fingerprints too) has an [ABA empirical analysis](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/antitrust_law/resources/source/2025-june/biometric-privacy-litigation/), so the facial subset is the un-mined part. **Score: 18/25** (a4 b3 c3 d4 e4).
