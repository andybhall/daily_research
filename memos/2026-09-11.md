# Dataset Hunt — 2026-09-11

**Frontier:** 4. Platform / API exhaust (app-store metadata, browser extensions, model marketplaces, ToS diffs, changelogs, review corpora)
**Verdict:** The safety-disclosure content of AI model cards is a live governance panel nobody has built — and my probe of the top open models shows they document benchmarks (60%) far more than safety (3%).

Third pass on frontier 4 (08-12 HF Hub metadata, Civitai, Pixiv; 09-01 MCP registries, AI Agent Marketplace, AUP tracking). Those mined *popularity* metadata and marketplaces. The un-mined corner is the **governance content** of the documentation itself — what model cards disclose about risk and safety, now that the EU AI Act mandates it.

## Top find: AI model-card safety disclosures (Hugging Face)

Every model on the Hub ships a **model card** (the README) — an incidentally-accumulated, continuously-edited corpus of what developers say about their models' training data, intended use, limitations, and safety. Its *content* (not the download counts from 08-12) is a governance disclosure panel: model × card-version × disclosure-field × time.

**AI nexus + agenda.** Bullseye **Governance** — voluntary (now partly mandated) AI safety/risk disclosure, the industry-side complement to our government AI registers (NL/UK/US). AI nexus intrinsic.

**Why it clears the bar.** Developers' real documentation choices under rising regulatory stakes — revealed behavior (a4); every model card accrues and is edited over years (2022→2026 in my sample), a byproduct of publishing, not assembled to be studied (b4); **partly claimed** — a Scholar check finds the AI Transparency Atlas (50 models) and a 2024 snapshot of 64k cards, but **no ongoing full-Hub disclosure panel**, and the post-EU-AI-Act view is fresh (c3); panel-able by model and card revision over time (d4); dead-on Governance (e5). **Score: 20/25** (a4 b4 c3 d4 e5).

**Evidence from a live sample.** I pulled the **60 most-downloaded text-generation models** and fetched **57 model-card READMEs** this run, scanning their headings. The gap is the story: **60% have an evaluation/benchmark section, but only 18% a bias/risks/limitations section and just 3% a safety/harm section** (43% intended-use, 15% training-data); median card 1,073 words; only 3/60 gated. Capability is documented; safety is not. This extends the 2024 finding that ~85% of 64k risk-sectioned cards lacked substantive risk info ([AI Transparency Atlas](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12443v1)), now against the [EU AI Act Article 50](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14758) machine-readable-disclosure duties enforceable from 2026-08-02. Artifacts in [`analysis/2026-09-11/`](../analysis/2026-09-11/SUMMARY.md). (Caveat: heading-keyword detection is a lower bound.)

**Access & cost.** Free HF API + raw READMEs (rate-limit-friendly); a weekend to score the full Hub and diff card revisions from `lastModified`/commit history. **Nearest work / difference:** the Atlas *scores* 50 models once; nobody tracks the whole Hub's disclosures longitudinally, or tests whether the EU AI Act actually moves safety-section prevalence. **Headline figure:** share of new models with a substantive safety/risk section, by month, with the Aug-2026 EU AI Act line marked.
