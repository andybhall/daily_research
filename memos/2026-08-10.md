# Dataset Hunt — 2026-08-10

**Frontier:** 2. Money trails (procurement, financial disclosures, Form 990s, lobbying registrations, grants, subawards, sole-source contracts)
**Verdict:** FARA foreign-agent filings — a 1942→2026 panel of who is paid to lobby Washington on behalf of which foreign government, bulk-downloadable and barely mined at the relationship level.

## Top find: FARA foreign-agent filings (DOJ eFile)

Every agent of a foreign principal engaged in US political activity must register and file under the 1938 Foreign Agents Registration Act — registrations, semi-annual supplements, short forms, and foreign-principal records, exposed via a public API and a daily bulk repository ([DOJ eFile API](https://efile.fara.gov/api); [FARA user's guide, Hye Young You](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41309-020-00100-1)).

**Why it clears the bar.** These are payments and lobbying contacts logged under real legal stakes (criminal exposure for non-registration) — revealed foreign-influence behavior, not stated preference (a). Filings accumulate incidentally across decades (b) and are deeply panel-able: the same registrants represent the same foreign principals across years (d). Maps to **Information** (foreign persuasion of US policy) and **Governance** (e).

**Unclaimed — honestly.** Not virgin: a small foreign-lobbying literature exists (You 2020; [Grotteria & Miller, "Foreign Influence in US Politics"](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/ssrn-4058658_acd82c32-e073-4e61-98c8-f7a7ce7938fe.pdf)) and OpenSecrets' Foreign Lobby Watch indexes the money. But the *relationship- and contact-level* data stays under-exploited precisely because the granular exhibits are scattered scanned PDFs — criterion c is the one discount (≈3/5).

**Evidence from a live sample.** I pulled the DOJ bulk registrant and foreign-principal tables today (via the OpenSanctions `us_fara_filings` mirror, refreshed 2026-08-10) and analyzed them: **7,075 registrants**, **17,724 foreign-principal relationships**, registrant dates spanning **1942–2026**, with **28% of registrants representing more than one foreign principal** (one represents 278) — the panel structure is real, not asserted. Top foreign-principal countries: **Japan 6.6%, Canada 4.6%, Mexico 3.3%, Great Britain 3.1%**, with USSR (1.7%) showing the historical depth; new foreign-principal registrations rise through the first Trump term (2017: 232 → 2019: 308). Script + full country table in [`analysis/2026-08-10/`](../analysis/2026-08-10/SUMMARY.md).

**Access & cost.** Bulk CSV/XML daily + JSON API, no auth; the structured registrant/principal tables are a weekend, but linking the *narrative activity exhibits* (who lobbied whom, on what bill) means OCR over scanned PDFs — a quarter. **Nearest work / our difference:** existing studies aggregate spend; nobody has built the registrant×foreign-principal×year influence panel to ask whether foreign lobbying *reallocates* toward the party in power after each election. **Headline figure:** a country→committee flow of foreign-agent contacts over 20 years. **Score: 22/25** (a5 b4 c3 d5 e5).

## Runner-ups

**India electoral bonds (SBI/ECI disclosure).** Court-ordered March-2024 release of the previously-anonymous corporate-to-party donation instrument: **22,217 bonds purchased Apr 2019–Feb 2024**, with purchaser (company) and redeemer (party) sides both disclosed ([ECI release](https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/ec-publishes-details-of-electoral-bond-data-on-its-website-see-full-list-124031401172_1.html); granular [SBI dataset on Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/shaundanielll/electoral-bond-data-state-bank-of-india)). Behaviorally revealed corporate money under (broken) anonymity, panel-able by company and party, freshly disclosed and academically near-untouched beyond ADR/journalism. Representation + Governance. Note: the aggregate [Dataful mirror](https://dataful.in/datasets/14716/) (894 rows) lacks donor→party linkage — the granular ECI/SBI tables are the prize. **Score: 22/25** (a5 b4 c4 d4 e5).

**GAO bid-protest decisions.** Firms formally contesting who wins federal contracts — GAO resolves **1,000+ protests a year**, issuing 380 merits decisions in FY25 ([GAO search](https://www.gao.gov/legal/bid-protests/search); [GAO-25-108652](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-108652)). Revealed behavior by losing bidders under real money stakes, panel-able by agency and repeat protester, and empirically under-mined. Governance. Access is a text/PDF corpus (GAO site + third-party [Tango API](https://www.makegov.com/blog/protests-endpoint)); not probed today to keep the pull bounded. **Score: 21/25** (a4 b4 c4 d4 e5).

## Shortlist

- **IRS 527 / Form 8872** — political-org contributions & expenditures, weekly bulk since 2000 ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/political-organizations/political-organization-filing-and-disclosure)). Panel-able, distinct from the FEC seed → **Found**.
- **World Bank debarment + Contract Awards (FY2017+)** — 1,386 sanctioned firms plus contract-award data ([WB](https://www.worldbank.org/en/projects-operations/procurement/debarred-firms)) → **Found**.
- **ProZorro (Ukraine open procurement)** — every tender/bid since 2015; scores high but is a flagship OCDS dataset with real prior use (CGD/DOZORRO) → Rejected (studied).
- **Brazil TSE campaign finance** (dadosabertos.tse.jus.br) — candidate/donor bulk, but corporate-donation effects already studied (Boas/Hidalgo/Richardson) → Rejected (studied).
- **IDB sanctioned-firms list** ([IADB Open Data](https://data.iadb.org/dataset/dataset-of-sanctioned-firms-and-individuals)) — cross-debarment companion to World Bank; small/administrative → shortlist only.
