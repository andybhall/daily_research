# Dataset Hunt — 2026-08-19

**Frontier:** 1. Legal / judicial exhaust (dockets, sanctions orders, amicus briefs, discovery exhibits, consent decrees, PACER/RECAP corners)
**Verdict:** Charlotin's AI Hallucination Cases database — every court decision worldwide that caught a lawyer filing AI-fabricated citations, exploding from 16 cases in 2023 to 1,013 in 2026, downloadable and barely analyzed.

The AI-focus gate turns legal exhaust into "AI *in* the courts." Three fresh, on-agenda finds surfaced, all Governance (how the legal system confronts AI).

## Top find: AI Hallucination Cases Database (Damien Charlotin)

A researcher-maintained, daily-updated database of every documented court/tribunal decision where a party was found to have relied on AI-hallucinated citations or arguments, worldwide, searchable by court, party, AI tool, and outcome ([site](https://www.damiencharlotin.com/hallucinations/); started April 2025 at HEC's Smart Law Hub).

**AI nexus + agenda.** Real judicial decisions sanctioning AI misuse — bullseye **Governance** (how courts detect and police AI in legal practice), AI nexus intrinsic.

**Why it clears the bar.** Lawyers using AI under real stakes and getting caught (a); genuine court-decision exhaust piling up as the phenomenon spreads (b); panel-able by court × jurisdiction × tool × legal field × time × penalty (d). **Unclaimed (c≈4):** enormous press and a Kaggle mirror, but rigorous academic panel analysis is thin (the DB is ~16 months old).

**Evidence from a live sample.** The site 403s generic fetchers, but a browser User-Agent returns a full **CSV export**, which I pulled and analyzed today: **1,934 cases, 18 fields, 41 jurisdictions**, with **351 carrying a recorded monetary penalty**. The growth curve is the story — **16 (2023) → 59 (2024) → 845 (2025) → 1,013 (2026)**. ChatGPT leads named tools (118; Claude 11, Perplexity 5, Copilot 5, Grok 4), though most entries are "Implied"; cases cluster in S.D.N.Y. (48) and in contract (445), administrative (251), civil-rights (194), and employment (172) law. Script + tables in [`analysis/2026-08-19/`](../analysis/2026-08-19/SUMMARY.md).

**Access & cost.** One CSV, no auth (browser UA); a weekend to build the panel. **Nearest work / difference:** journalism counts cases; nobody has modeled *which* courts, practice areas, and tools drive sanctions, or whether penalties deter repeat filings. **Headline figure:** monthly hallucination-sanction filings by legal field and AI tool, 2023→2026. **Score: 24/25** (a5 b5 c4 d5 e5).

## Runner-ups

**AI copyright/IP litigation dockets.** 70+ infringement suits by rights-holders against AI companies, tracked case-by-case ([ChatGPT Is Eating the World](https://chatgptiseatingtheworld.com/2025/10/08/status-of-all-51-copyright-lawsuits-v-ai-oct-8-2025-no-more-decisions-on-fair-use-in-2025/); [Mishcon GenAI IP tracker](https://www.mishcon.com/generative-ai-intellectual-property-cases-and-policy-tracker)). Behaviorally revealed litigation under enormous stakes (the $1.5B Bartz v. Anthropic settlement, ~$3,000/work), panel-able by case × court × time, Governance. Structured as law-firm lists today; the docket-level panel (via PACER/RECAP) is unbuilt. **Score: 21/25** (a5 b4 c3 d4 e5).

**Judicial AI standing orders.** 300+ judges now require AI-use disclosure/certification in filings, with 113+ active orders tracked ([RAILS/Ropes & Gray/Bloomberg trackers](https://trace.law/kb/court-ai-disclosure-orders)); Judge Starr's May-2023 order (post-*Mata v. Avianca*) was the first. Fresh AI-native governance exhaust, panel-able by judge/court × time — how the judiciary is writing its own AI rules. Trackers are partly commercial; RAILS is open. **Score: 20/25** (a4 b4 c3 d4 e5).
