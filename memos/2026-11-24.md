**Verdict:** Null day — nothing cleared the bar.

Eighth pass on frontier 8 (08-26 union AI provisions / NLRB; 09-15 NYC LL144 AEDT audits; 09-25 Worker Info Exchange + DOL LCA AI; 10-05 WageIndicator CBA AI clauses; 10-15, 11-04 null). Third consecutive null. The on-agenda AI-labor objects are logged, the watch items haven't moved, and the fresh candidates are unlabeled general datasets or proprietary analyses.

The disclosure-registry watch has not moved. Uruguay's **Law 20.396** is a platform-worker statute whose Article 4 requires platforms to disclose automated monitoring/decision systems — but it is not a public registry, and no 2026 disclosure data has been published; the registries elsewhere remain proposals (Argentina's National Registry of Digital Platforms was still under deliberation as of Feb 2026) ([pdelc](https://www.pdelc.com.uy/en/news/labor/ilo-approved-convention-on-work-based-on-digital-platforms/); [IBA](https://www.ibanet.org/between-algorithms-and-fundamental-rights-EU-AI-Act)). Same posture as 11-04 — a future on-agenda find on publication, nothing to pull today.

The strike data has no AI nexus to pull. Cornell's **ILR-LER Labor Action Tracker** is a genuine work-stoppage panel (303 actions in 2025; pay/first-contract/healthcare the top demands), but its published coding has **no AI or automation demand category** — the AI-strike story (WGA/SAG-AFTRA, dockworkers) is journalism, not a coded field, so any AI nexus is build-it-yourself keyword work, and it's adjacent to the logged union-AI finds (08-26) ([LAT 2025](https://www.ilr.cornell.edu/faculty-and-research/labor-action-tracker-2025); [methodology](https://striketracker.ilr.cornell.edu/methodology.html)).

The AI-hiring-discrimination corner has no open dataset and is shrinking. There is no public EEOC charge-level dataset of AI-tool complaints, and federal disparate-impact enforcement — the basis of most AI-bias theories — is retreating: a Sept 2025 memo closed disparate-impact charges and the EEOC withdrew its 2022/2023 AI guidance ([Daily Record](https://thedailyrecord.com/2025/10/01/eeoc-disparate-impact-ai-discrimination/)). The live action is private litigation (Mobley v. Workday), which already sits in the logged DAIL AI-litigation tracker (09-08) ([Law360](https://www.law360.com/articles/2420196/ai-battle-headlines-workplace-bias-cases-to-watch-in-2026)).

Worker AI-sentiment is proprietary, not a deposit. Glassdoor's economic-research team reports AI mentions in reviews up 240% YoY and the positive share falling from 81% (2019) to 43% (mid-2026), with job-insecurity mentions up 63% ([the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-sentiment-is-turning-sour-as-employee-reviews-reveal-growing-frustration-across-the-workforce/)) — but this is Glassdoor's own analysis of its proprietary review corpus (a logged seed), not an open coded dataset, and Blind's figures are stated-preference surveys, not exhaust.

*Meta-run note:* third straight frontier-8 null (last find 11-18, World Bank AI procurement, frontier 2). The AI-labor objects — collectively-bargained AI clauses (US + intl), NLRB disputes, LL144 audits, gig-worker SARs, H-1B AI filings, AI-attributed layoffs — are logged; the live prospects are the workplace-AI disclosure registries on publication (Uruguay/CA AB 1898/Canada) and CA SB 951 WARN AI-layoff fields (Jan 2027). Same posture; flagging, not editing the rotation.
