## Top find: Political Deepfakes Incident Database (PDID)

A researcher-coded database of politically-salient deepfakes and cheapfakes (GRAIL Lab; Schiff, Schiff, Bueno et al.), introduced at AAAI 2024 ([paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/30349); [project](https://www.grail-lab.org/project/political-deepfakes-incident-database)). As of Oct 2025 it holds **939 images and 502 videos, 2018→present**, each coded for target, presented-as-real/fake, external verification, depicted harms, propagation metrics, sharer info, and manipulation architecture/modality.

**AI nexus + agenda.** AI-generated political disinformation aimed at real elections and figures — bullseye **Information** (how political information is distorted), AI nexus intrinsic.

**Why it clears the bar.** Real incidents deployed under real political stakes (a); panel-able by target × time × platform, with propagation metadata (d); squarely on-agenda (e5). The honest discounts: it is a **curated incident collection**, not incidentally-accumulated exhaust (b≈3), and it is a published dataset, though large-N analysis using it is still thin (c≈3).

**Evidence — could not probe.** The database is hosted on Airtable and open access is "pending consideration of ethical risks"; no public CSV/API was confirmed today. So the figures above come from pages fetched this run, not a live pull — logged honestly in [`analysis/2026-08-18/`](../analysis/2026-08-18/SUMMARY.md). **Access & cost:** request-based today; a weekend once the export opens. **Nearest work / difference:** the intro paper describes the DB; nobody has used its propagation + manipulation-architecture coding as a panel of *how* political deepfakes spread and evolve across the 2024 global election year. **Headline figure:** monthly deepfake incidents by target and manipulation modality, 2018→2026. **Score: 19/25** (a4 b3 c3 d4 e5).

## Runner-ups

**Gen-Review — LLM penetration of peer review.** A parallel corpus of human- and AI-written peer reviews from ICLR-era venues, publicly deposited on **Harvard Dataverse (CC-BY-4.0)** ([DOI 10.7910/DVN/PYDPEZ](https://doi.org/10.7910/DVN/PYDPEZ); [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21192)). Genuinely incidental (real reviews that happened to be AI-written) and panel-able over time. **Partial probe:** via the Dataverse API today I confirmed one file, `gen_review.db`, **~1.6 GB SQLite, CC-BY** — but a bounded 1.5 MB range GET couldn't reach the schema, and the full DB exceeds a polite pull, so fields/counts stay unverified. Weakest on political-directness (integrity of the epistemic commons, not electoral politics), so e≈3. **Score: 18/25** (a4 b4 c3 d4 e3).

**Human–AI political conversations.** "Talking Politics with AI" ([arXiv 2607.00551](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00551)) classifies **4.30M** real human-AI chats and finds **3.9% are political** — mostly practical (info-seeking, drafting), not opinion. The most genuinely *incidental*, on-agenda object (AI reshaping how citizens seek political information), but the underlying corpora (WildChat/LMSYS/ShareLM) are already claimed, so this is a pointer to an under-worked subset rather than a fresh deposit (c≈2). **Score: 18/25** (a4 b4 c2 d3 e4).
