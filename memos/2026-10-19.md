# Dataset Hunt — 2026-10-19

**Frontier:** 2. Money trails (procurement, financial disclosures, Form 990s, lobbying registrations, grants, subawards, sole-source contracts)
**Verdict:** Null day — nothing cleared the bar.

Sixth pass on frontier 2 (08-10 FARA/electoral bonds; 08-20 USASpending AI procurement + LDA lobbying + Open Phil philanthropy; 09-09 Leading the Future super PAC + Good Jobs First data-center subsidies; 09-29 null; 10-09 null). The clean open AI-money datasets are now comprehensively logged across procurement, lobbying (US + EU), philanthropy, campaign finance, subsidies, and foreign agents — and the frontier was null on both prior visits. Rather than re-screen the candidates I already rejected (Gulf SWF money, AI-safety funding, AI-vendor contracts, FEC donor giving), I dug into the one money sub-territory five visits never touched — and it doesn't quite clear.

**Federal AI research grants** (assistance awards, not contracts) are a genuinely distinct object from the logged procurement slice: the government *funding* the AI frontier rather than *buying* AI. The anchors are real — the NSF National AI Research Institutes (27 institutes across 40+ states, ~$500M, up to $20M each; [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Artificial_Intelligence_Research_Institutes)), the NAIRR Operations Center (~$100M), and the June 2026 DARPA–NSF **AI Forge** funding interpretability, AI-control, and adversarial-robustness research for national security ([GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/blog/darpa-nsf-ai-forge-rfi-june-22-2026-deadline-750k-3m-project-ventures-interpretability-control-adversarial-robustness-strategy)). I verified the data is pullable: a live query to the open [NSF award API](https://api.nsf.gov/services/v1/awards.json) returns AI-keyworded awards, and USASpending's assistance-award API carries the same. But three things keep it off the ledger. The AI nexus is **loose and messy to define** — the first NSF award my probe returned merely *mentions* AI in a math-conference abstract, so an "AI grant" panel needs heavy hand-curation. The data source (NSF / USASpending) is **maximally mined** in science-of-science research. And research funding leans **science/innovation policy**, not the political-information/representation/governance core this agenda names — the agenda's Governance list includes "AI procurement" (why 08-20 fit) but not research funding. Distinct and probe-able, but science-policy-adjacent on an over-mined source: a shortlist, not a find.

Everything else was logged or commercial. The AI-lobbying trackers **The AI Lobby** and **OpenLobby** ([OpenLobby](https://www.openlobby.us/investigations/ai-lobbying-q3-2026)) repackage Senate LDA filings — the object logged on 08-20 — as advocacy dashboards with conflicting estimates ($225M vs. $1.2B through Q3). The 2026-cycle campaign money (Leading the Future's $100M+, the ~$83M NYT figure) is the super-PAC find from 09-09 refreshed with Q3 data.

*Meta-run flag:* tenth straight null, all on 5th–6th-visit frontiers (last finds 10-02, 10-05). I've shifted to digging a *new sub-territory* per repeat visit rather than re-screening — today that surfaced federal AI grants, a genuine new candidate that fell just short on agenda-fit. Recommend the meta-run formalize this (named sub-territory per visit; widen to sub-national / non-English / older-unexploited) and consider lengthening the revisit cycle.
