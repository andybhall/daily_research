# Dataset Hunt — 2026-08-09

**Frontier:** 1. Legal / judicial exhaust (dockets, sanctions orders, amicus briefs, discovery exhibits, consent decrees, PACER/RECAP corners)
**Verdict:** The Opioid Industry Documents Archive — millions of internal corporate records disgorged in discovery, bulk-open on AWS and barely touched quantitatively.

## Top find: Opioid Industry Documents Archive (OIDA)

Millions of previously-internal documents — "company emails, memos, presentations, sales reports, budgets, audit reports, Drug Enforcement Administration briefings, meeting agendas and minutes, expert witness reports, and depositions of company executives" — made public through opioid **litigation settlements and judgments**, curated by UCSF + Johns Hopkins ([UCSF launch](https://www.library.ucsf.edu/news/launch-opioid-industry-documents-archive/)). Individual releases are already huge: **1.4M documents from Mallinckrodt** and **114k from McKinsey** ([JHU](https://publichealth.jhu.edu/2022/opioid-industry-archive-releases-1-4-million-documents-from-leading-opioid-maker-implicated-in-drug-crisis)).

**Why it clears the bar.** This is discovery exhaust in its purest form: business records created incidentally under real commercial stakes, then disgorged by courts — revealed corporate conduct, not stated preference (criteria a, b). It is panel-able by firm (Purdue, Mallinckrodt, McKinsey, distributors) across years, and maps to **Governance** (corporate conduct + regulatory capture of the DEA) and **Information** (corporate political strategy) — criterion e.

**Unclaimed — honestly.** Not virgin: a *Social Science & Medicine* 2023 piece sketches "new directions in research on corporate political strategy" ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0955395923000464)) and an AAAI 2026 paper built an AI benchmark, OIDA-QA, on 400k docs ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09914)). But that's a methods benchmark and a directions essay; the corpus of millions remains almost entirely unexploited as a *quantitative firm×year influence panel* — criterion c is the one discount (≈3/5).

**Access & cost.** Bulk-open on the AWS Registry of Open Data — metadata, source files (PDF/Excel/PPT) and OCR text in a public S3 bucket, no account needed (`aws s3 ls --no-sign-request s3://opioid-industry-documents-archive-dataset-bucket/`), **updated monthly** ([AWS](https://registry.opendata.aws/oida/)). Collection is already done; the real cost is document classification + storage — a subset (McKinsey's 114k) is a weekend, the full archive a quarter. **Nearest work / our difference:** existing work is qualitative or an ML benchmark; nobody has built a firm×year panel of *influence events* (regulator contacts, sales-quota escalations, consultant playbooks). **Headline figure:** per-firm timeline of documented regulator-contact and sales-pressure events overlaid on shipment and overdose-death curves. **First analysis:** within-firm event study — after a DEA enforcement threat, does documented lobbying/consulting activity spike? Identified off enforcement-action timing.

**Score: 22/25** (a5 b5 c3 d4 e5).

## Runner-up: DataJud — Brazil's National Judiciary Database (CNJ public API)

Procedural metadata and docket movements for cases across **every instance of the Brazilian judiciary** (STJ, TST, TSE, STM, all state/regional courts), systematically collected since 2009, with >98.9% of new cases filed electronically, exposed through a public Elasticsearch-backed API ([CNJ API wiki](https://datajud-wiki.cnj.jus.br/api-publica/); [ACM ICEGOV](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3614321.3614381)). Bureaucratic exhaust at national scale: case classes, subjects, and movement histories piling up (criteria a4, b5). Panel-able by court/case-type/month; Governance layer. Prior use is real but mostly administrative-transparency and Portuguese-language ([DGO2025](https://proceedings.open.tudelft.nl/DGO2025/article/download/993/1024/1396)); it is under-exploited as a *behavioral* substrate — litigant strategy, delay, appeal cascades (c≈3). Terms of use explicitly permit academic research. **First analysis:** staggered-DiD on electronic-filing/PJe rollout across courts → disposition time. **Score: 21/25** (a4 b5 c3 d4 e5).
