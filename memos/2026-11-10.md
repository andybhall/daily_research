Fourth consecutive null here. The marketplace/metadata corners are logged, and the two fresh angles — model-release metadata and AI election-policy changes — are off-agenda or un-deposited.

The fresh platform data is off-agenda. The **LLM Gateway release timeline** is a continuously-updated open dataset of **463 models from 55 providers** with release dates ([LLM Gateway](https://llmgateway.io/timeline)), but it's model-release metadata with no political nexus; the other hits (Kaggle, Opendatabay, LiveRamp, Ollama, Civitai [logged 08-12]) are general data/model marketplaces.

The on-agenda object has no dataset. AI companies rewrote their election policies for the midterms — **Anthropic's 2026 Usage Policy update** renamed the elections section "Do Not Undermine Democratic Processes" and **dropped its blanket ban on personalized vote/campaign targeting** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/2026-usage-policy-update)); **OpenAI's election-safeguards page** (updated Oct 20) keeps its ban on scaled campaign messaging and launched an Election Operations Center ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/election-safeguards-2026/)); Google, Meta, and others vary ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/02/nx-s1-6025407/chatbots-midterms-voting-advice)). This is a genuine platform-governance object (ToS/changelog diffs on political AI use), but there is **no open dataset tracking these changes over time** — the diffs live on the policy pages, so it's a build-from-page-archives project — and it's the same object as the AUP tracking logged 09-01 and the Midas Watchtower screened 10-01. The Brennan Center's finding that Gemini and Meta AI grew *more* willing to generate fake election images is an audit result, not a dataset.
