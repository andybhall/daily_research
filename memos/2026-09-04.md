## Top find: EU AI-lobbying / Big Tech influence on the AI Act

The EU Transparency Register plus published Commission-official meeting records make Brussels the most transparent lobbying arena in the world — and Integritywatch EU combines both into a daily-updated dataset ([Integritywatch](https://www.integritywatch.eu/ecmeetings.php); [LobbyFacts](https://www.lobbyfacts.eu/); analysis by [Corporate Europe Observatory](https://corporateeurope.org/en/2025/10/revealed-tech-industry-now-spending-record-eu151-million-lobbying-eu)).

**AI nexus + agenda.** Who shapes the world's flagship AI law — bullseye **Governance** (AI-industry influence on AI regulation), non-US, AI nexus intrinsic.

**Why it clears the bar.** Real lobbying meetings and spend under high regulatory stakes (a); meeting disclosures accreting continuously via the Transparency Register (b); panel-able by organisation × official × meeting × topic × time (d); on-agenda (e5). **Unclaimed (c≈3):** watchdogs (CEO, LobbyControl, SOMO) document it in narrative reports and a "[249 capture mechanisms](https://www.somo.nl/how-big-tech-framed-eu-choices/)" coding, but no rigorous meeting-level AI-influence panel linking access to AI Act outcomes exists.

**Evidence — partial probe.** Verified this run: tech-industry EU lobby spend hit a record **€151M** (+33.6% since 2023); in H1 2025 **AI was the single most-lobbied topic — 58 Commission meetings, 40% of all**; Big Tech held **378 meetings with senior Commission officials and MEPs** (>1 per working day); in 2023, **78% of high-level AI meetings were with corporate interests**. The catch: the EU Transparency Register REST API was unreachable here (HTTP 000) and Integritywatch is JS-driven, so no clean live pull today — the data is downloadable via Integritywatch/LobbyFacts. Logged in [`analysis/2026-09-04/`](../analysis/2026-09-04/SUMMARY.md). Distinct from the US LDA (08-20) and FARA (08-10) finds. **Score: 21/25** (a5 b4 c3 d4 e5).

**Access & cost.** Transparency Register + Commission meeting open data + LobbyFacts bulk; a few weeks to build the AI-file meeting panel. **Nearest work / difference:** watchdogs tally meetings; nobody has modeled whether lobbying *access* predicts which AI Act provisions get watered down. **Headline figure:** AI-file Commission meetings by actor type (corporate vs. civil society) against each AI Act amendment, 2021→2026.

## Runner-ups

**OECD.AI national AI policy database.** A live repository of **900+ national AI policies and initiatives across 80+ jurisdictions**, maintained by official country focal points ([OECD.AI dashboards](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/national)). The canonical comparative AI-governance panel (country × policy × time); non-US and on-agenda, though curated (assembled) and lightly used academically ([arXiv 2507.05400](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.05400)). **Score: 18/25** (a3 b3 c3 d4 e5).

**EU GPAI Code of Practice signatories.** The EU AI Office publishes who signed its voluntary General-Purpose AI Code of Practice — **26 companies by Aug 2025** (Amazon, Anthropic, Google, IBM, Microsoft, OpenAI, Mistral, Aleph Alpha…), with **Meta refusing, xAI signing only the Safety & Security chapter, and Chinese firms absent** ([EU signatory taskforce](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/signatory-taskforce-gpai-code-practice)). A revealed compliance dataset of AI self-regulation (company × chapter × sign/refuse × date). **Score: 18/25** (a4 b3 c3 d3 e5).
