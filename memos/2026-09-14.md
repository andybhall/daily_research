## Top find: Canada's Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA) registry

Under the Treasury Board [Directive on Automated Decision-Making](https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset?q=algorithmic+impact+assessment), every federal department must complete and **publish an AIA** — a mandatory questionnaire scoring an automated system's impact — before deploying it. The completed AIAs sit on the Open Government portal in PDF/JSON/XLS under an open licence.

**AI nexus + agenda.** Bullseye **Governance** — algorithmic decision-making inside government, self-disclosed. Non-US, distinct instrument. The AIA is a *scored* risk self-assessment, richer than the descriptive NL/UK/EU registers already in the ledger.

**Why it clears the bar.** Real, mandated risk disclosures naming the system, the department, and the responsible official — revealed behavior under compliance stakes (a4); the register accretes as departments deploy systems, 2020→2026, a byproduct of the Directive, not assembled to be studied (b4); **lightly claimed** — one qualitative critique exists ([arXiv 2604.15514](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.15514)) but nobody has mined the structured multi-AIA panel (c3); panel-able by system × department × year × impact-field (d4); dead-on Governance (e5). **Score: 20/25** (a4 b4 c3 d4 e5).

**Evidence from a live sample.** I queried the open.canada.ca CKAN API and pulled 10 AIA JSONs this run: **30 published AIAs**, growing 2020→2026 (2 → 6 → 7 → 8 → 4 by year). The concentration is the story — they cluster in the most consequential decisions on people: **Immigration (IRCC) 7, Employment & Social Development (ESDC) 7, Border Services (CBSA) 4, Veterans Affairs 3**. Each AIA is a **~79-field coded questionnaire** (respondent, project phase, risk profile, `impact1–14`), with answers coded `item{choice}-{points}` — e.g. a Veterans Affairs mental-health-benefit tool self-described as "no impact on rights/freedoms." Artifacts in [`analysis/2026-09-14/`](../analysis/2026-09-14/SUMMARY.md). (The official impact level I–IV is computed from the item scores — the build step, not claimed here.)

**Access & cost.** Free CKAN API + JSON per AIA; a weekend to parse all 30 into a system × question matrix, recompute impact levels, and diff raw-vs-mitigation scores. **Nearest work / difference:** the one paper reads the register qualitatively; nobody has quantified what departments admit — which impact dimensions they flag, where mitigation most reduces scores, whether higher-stakes domains file *lower* self-scores. **Headline figure:** self-assessed impact level by decision sector (immigration/benefits/border) over 2020→2026.
