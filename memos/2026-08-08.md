## Top find: EU Public Consultations & Feedback Portal — full-text corpus (Zenodo 15864330)

A complete scrape of every public input to EU legislation on the Commission's "Have Your Say" portal **through May 2025** — initiative/consultation metadata, the attached files, and extracted text for *every* attachment, organized across 38 policy topics; 37.8 GB compressed, ~9.6 TB total, CC-BY-4.0, deposited by Mario Angst (UZH) on 11 Jul 2025 via his `eu_consultations` scraper ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/15864330); [package](https://github.com/marioangst/eu_consultations)).

**Why it clears the bar.** The attachments are position papers that firms, trade associations, NGOs and member states write to move binding EU rules — optimized persuasion under real stakes, not survey answers (criterion a). The portal accumulates incidentally, initiative after initiative, and the *same* organisations recur across consultations and DGs, giving a stakeholder × initiative panel (criteria b, d). It maps cleanly to **Governance** (how EU rules get contested) and **Information** (organised persuasion of regulators) — criterion e.

**Unclaimed — honestly.** EU consultation *text* is not virgin: there is an active literature on it (Klüver-style quantitative text analysis; [JEPP 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13501763.2024.2320836); [Green-Deal lobbying, Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41309-025-00253-x)). But that work uses hand-built *slices*; nobody has exploited this **comprehensive full-portal corpus with the attachment files extracted to text** — the 9.6 TB of PDFs/DOCX is the novel, unworked layer. Criterion c is the one discount (≈3/5).

**Access & cost.** Open Zenodo download, no auth, docling-JSON text already extracted; a topic-subset is a weekend, the full corpus needs real storage. **Nearest work** ([Angst's descriptor](https://zenodo.org/records/15864330); the 2026 "Traceable by Design" LLM pipeline, [arXiv 2605.30995](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.30995)) builds tooling/dashboards; our angle is a *stakeholder-influence panel* — does an interest group's textual position predict the final rule text, and does recurrence across consultations buy influence? **Headline figure:** a bipartite map of organisations → policy topics over a decade, sized by submission volume, showing which coalitions saturate which DGs. **First analysis:** within-initiative, does business-side submission volume/positioning move regulatory outcomes vs. NGO input — identified off consultation-timing and topic fixed effects.

**Score: 22/25** (a5 b4 c3 d5 e5).

## Runner-up: Mexican municipal-elections precinct database (Nature Sci Data, 2025)

Precinct-level returns for Mexican municipal elections **1994–2019**: 456,051 precinct returns, 15,581 municipal elections, 62,341 precincts, 2,033 municipalities across 31 states, with coalition/party vote shares (PRI/PAN/PRD/MORENA), turnout, registered voters, and *lagged* incumbent-coalition data; compiled from state electoral-institute sites and FOI requests, standardized in R, released CC-BY-NC-ND on Zenodo + GitHub ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04918-9); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11976933/)). Behaviorally revealed (votes under stakes), incidental, deeply panel-able — the descriptor explicitly enables "panel and regression discontinuity" and municipal DiD designs. Distinct from the ledger's US-local and Malaysian corpora. The discount is the same as its US cousin: election-returns is a well-worn genre and precinct-level Mexican RD has prior use, so it's a foundational-but-known object. **Score: 22/25** (a4 b5 c3 d5 e5) — ranked below the EU corpus on novelty/behavioral richness.
