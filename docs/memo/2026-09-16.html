How corporate boards say they oversee AI

A count, from SEC proxy filings, of how many company boards now explicitly disclose that they oversee the firm's AI — the governance structure behind the AI, tracked year by year.

Third pass on frontier 9 (08-17 AI shareholder resolutions + proxy votes, Manifold, Metaculus; 08-27 10-K AI-risk disclosures, AI market-agents, Bittensor). Two legs of corporate AI governance are logged — what shareholders demand and what firms flag as risk. The un-mined leg is what boards say they do: the oversight structure itself.

Top find: Board AI-oversight & director-AI-expertise disclosures (proxy statements)

US public companies disclose their governance structure in the annual DEF 14A proxy. A new, distinct object is emerging there: explicit statements that the board (or a committee) oversees the company's AI, and that named directors have AI expertise. It's separate from the shareholder resolutions (08-17) and 10-K risk factors (08-27) already in the ledger — the supply side of AI governance, not the demand or risk side.

AI nexus + agenda. Bullseye Governance — how corporate boards actually govern AI, self-disclosed under investor and legal stakes. AI nexus intrinsic.

Why it clears the bar. Mandatory governance disclosures under real stakes — revealed behavior (a4); proxy filings accrue annually and are EDGAR-full-text-searchable back two decades, a byproduct not assembled to be studied (b4); lightly claimed — ISS-Corporate and Harvard corpgov report the headline percentages, but no open EDGAR panel of board-AI-oversight content exists (c3); panel-able by company × year × oversight-structure (d4); dead-on Governance (e5). Score: 20/25 (a4 b4 c3 d4 e5).

Evidence from a live sample. I queried EDGAR full-text search this run for the phrase "oversight of artificial intelligence" in DEF 14A filings, by year: 0 through 2022 → 1 (2023) → 3 (2024) → 23 (2025) → 57 (2026) — near-absent to standard in three years. The stricter "board oversight of artificial intelligence" returns 13 in 2026. For context, ISS-Corporate reports directors' AI-expertise disclosure rose from 26% of the Fortune 100 in 2024 to 44% in 2025 (Harvard corpgov). This is the governance-structure subset of the ~1,432 proxies merely mentioning AI (this hunt's 08-17 probe). Artifacts in analysis/2026-09-16/. (Phrase counts are a lower bound.)

Access & cost. Free EDGAR full-text search + bulk DEF 14A downloads; a weekend to classify board/committee AI-oversight charters and director-skills matrices into a company × year panel. Nearest work / difference: ISS-Corporate reports aggregate percentages; nobody has built the open, filing-level panel or tested whether disclosed board oversight predicts fewer AI-risk incidents or lower shareholder-resolution pressure. Headline figure: share of S&P 500 boards disclosing AI oversight by year, split by which committee (audit vs. technology vs. full board) holds it.
