The disclosure-registry watch has not moved. Same posture as 11-04 — a future on-agenda find on publication, nothing to pull today.

The strike data has no AI nexus to pull. Cornell's ILR-LER Labor Action Tracker is a genuine work-stoppage panel (303 actions in 2025; pay/first-contract/healthcare the top demands), but its published coding has no AI or automation demand category — the AI-strike story (WGA/SAG-AFTRA, dockworkers) is journalism, not a coded field, so any AI nexus is build-it-yourself keyword work, and it's adjacent to the logged union-AI finds (08-26) (LAT 2025; methodology).

The AI-hiring-discrimination corner has no open dataset and is shrinking. There is no public EEOC charge-level dataset of AI-tool complaints, and federal disparate-impact enforcement — the basis of most AI-bias theories — is retreating: a Sept 2025 memo closed disparate-impact charges and the EEOC withdrew its 2022/2023 AI guidance (Daily Record). The live action is private litigation (Mobley v. Workday), which already sits in the logged DAIL AI-litigation tracker (09-08) (Law360).

Worker AI-sentiment is proprietary, not a deposit. Glassdoor's economic-research team reports AI mentions in reviews up 240% YoY and the positive share falling from 81% (2019) to 43% (mid-2026), with job-insecurity mentions up 63% (the-decoder) — but this is Glassdoor's own analysis of its proprietary review corpus (a logged seed), not an open coded dataset, and Blind's figures are stated-preference surveys, not exhaust.
