Big Tech lobbying on the EU AI Act

Records of who lobbies the European Commission on AI — the meetings, organizations, and spend behind the EU AI Act.

Revisiting frontier 7 (08-25 took China's CAC registry, European public-sector AI registers, India AI-in-elections). Today's distinct object: the politics of AI governance abroad — lobbying, comparative policy, and voluntary-code compliance.

Top find: EU AI-lobbying / Big Tech influence on the AI Act

The EU Transparency Register plus published Commission-official meeting records make Brussels the most transparent lobbying arena in the world — and Integritywatch EU combines both into a daily-updated dataset (Integritywatch; LobbyFacts; analysis by Corporate Europe Observatory).

AI nexus + agenda. Who shapes the world's flagship AI law — bullseye Governance (AI-industry influence on AI regulation), non-US, AI nexus intrinsic.

Why it clears the bar. Real lobbying meetings and spend under high regulatory stakes (a); meeting disclosures accreting continuously via the Transparency Register (b); panel-able by organisation × official × meeting × topic × time (d); on-agenda (e5). Unclaimed (c≈3): watchdogs (CEO, LobbyControl, SOMO) document it in narrative reports and a "249 capture mechanisms" coding, but no rigorous meeting-level AI-influence panel linking access to AI Act outcomes exists.

Evidence — partial probe. Verified this run: tech-industry EU lobby spend hit a record €151M (+33.6% since 2023); in H1 2025 AI was the single most-lobbied topic — 58 Commission meetings, 40% of all; Big Tech held 378 meetings with senior Commission officials and MEPs (>1 per working day); in 2023, 78% of high-level AI meetings were with corporate interests. The catch: the EU Transparency Register REST API was unreachable here (HTTP 000) and Integritywatch is JS-driven, so no clean live pull today — the data is downloadable via Integritywatch/LobbyFacts. Logged in analysis/2026-09-04/. Distinct from the US LDA (08-20) and FARA (08-10) finds. Score: 21/25 (a5 b4 c3 d4 e5).

Access & cost. Transparency Register + Commission meeting open data + LobbyFacts bulk; a few weeks to build the AI-file meeting panel. Nearest work / difference: watchdogs tally meetings; nobody has modeled whether lobbying access predicts which AI Act provisions get watered down. Headline figure: AI-file Commission meetings by actor type (corporate vs. civil society) against each AI Act amendment, 2021→2026.

Runner-ups

OECD.AI national AI policy database. A live repository of 900+ national AI policies and initiatives across 80+ jurisdictions, maintained by official country focal points (OECD.AI dashboards). The canonical comparative AI-governance panel (country × policy × time); non-US and on-agenda, though curated (assembled) and lightly used academically (arXiv 2507.05400). Score: 18/25 (a3 b3 c3 d4 e5).

EU GPAI Code of Practice signatories. The EU AI Office publishes who signed its voluntary General-Purpose AI Code of Practice — 26 companies by Aug 2025 (Amazon, Anthropic, Google, IBM, Microsoft, OpenAI, Mistral, Aleph Alpha…), with Meta refusing, xAI signing only the Safety & Security chapter, and Chinese firms absent (EU signatory taskforce). A revealed compliance dataset of AI self-regulation (company × chapter × sign/refuse × date). Score: 18/25 (a4 b3 c3 d3 e5).
