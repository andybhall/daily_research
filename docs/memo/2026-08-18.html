The AI-focus gate plus this frontier's "incidental deposit" requirement is a tight vise: genuinely incidental AI exhaust (real human-AI chats, AI text in the wild) is mostly in already-claimed corpora, while the fresh political-AI deposits are hand-curated or gated. I surface the honest best and log what's blocked.

Top find: Political Deepfakes Incident Database (PDID)

A researcher-coded database of politically-salient deepfakes and cheapfakes (GRAIL Lab; Schiff, Schiff, Bueno et al.), introduced at AAAI 2024 (paper; project). As of Oct 2025 it holds 939 images and 502 videos, 2018→present, each coded for target, presented-as-real/fake, external verification, depicted harms, propagation metrics, sharer info, and manipulation architecture/modality.

AI nexus + agenda. AI-generated political disinformation aimed at real elections and figures — bullseye Information (how political information is distorted), AI nexus intrinsic.

Why it clears the bar. Real incidents deployed under real political stakes (a); panel-able by target × time × platform, with propagation metadata (d); squarely on-agenda (e5). The honest discounts: it is a curated incident collection, not incidentally-accumulated exhaust (b≈3), and it is a published dataset, though large-N analysis using it is still thin (c≈3).

Evidence — could not probe. The database is hosted on Airtable and open access is "pending consideration of ethical risks"; no public CSV/API was confirmed today. So the figures above come from pages fetched this run, not a live pull — logged honestly in analysis/2026-08-18/. Access & cost: request-based today; a weekend once the export opens. Nearest work / difference: the intro paper describes the DB; nobody has used its propagation + manipulation-architecture coding as a panel of how political deepfakes spread and evolve across the 2024 global election year. Headline figure: monthly deepfake incidents by target and manipulation modality, 2018→2026. Score: 19/25 (a4 b3 c3 d4 e5).

Runner-ups

Gen-Review — LLM penetration of peer review. A parallel corpus of human- and AI-written peer reviews from ICLR-era venues, publicly deposited on Harvard Dataverse (CC-BY-4.0) (DOI 10.7910/DVN/PYDPEZ; paper). Genuinely incidental (real reviews that happened to be AI-written) and panel-able over time. Partial probe: via the Dataverse API today I confirmed one file, gen_review.db, ~1.6 GB SQLite, CC-BY — but a bounded 1.5 MB range GET couldn't reach the schema, and the full DB exceeds a polite pull, so fields/counts stay unverified. Weakest on political-directness (integrity of the epistemic commons, not electoral politics), so e≈3. Score: 18/25 (a4 b4 c3 d4 e3).

Human–AI political conversations. "Talking Politics with AI" (arXiv 2607.00551) classifies 4.30M real human-AI chats and finds 3.9% are political — mostly practical (info-seeking, drafting), not opinion. The most genuinely incidental, on-agenda object (AI reshaping how citizens seek political information), but the underlying corpora (WildChat/LMSYS/ShareLM) are already claimed, so this is a pointer to an under-worked subset rather than a fresh deposit (c≈2). Score: 18/25 (a4 b4 c2 d3 e4).
