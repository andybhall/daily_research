Top find: Hugging Face Hub metadata exhaust

The Hub's public API exposes, for every public model/dataset/Space, a rich record — downloads, likes, author, createdAt/lastModified, pipeline_tag, library_name, gated flag, license tags, and file lists (Hub API docs). This is the incidental record of who builds, licenses, and adopts open AI, accumulating continuously.

AI nexus + agenda. This is the AI supply side — how models are produced, licensed, and governed. Maps to Information (the production of AI systems that generate political content) and Governance (open-model licensing and access control). AI nexus is intrinsic, not bolted on.

Why it clears the bar. Downloads/likes are revealed adoption under real stakes (a); the metadata piles up incidentally (b); models and authors recur, with download/like trajectories over time (d, panel-able). Unclaimed (c≈3): model-card and "model proliferation" studies exist (model-card metadata, 2025), but the Hub as a behavioral panel — adoption trajectories, author concentration, license drift, fine-tune lineage — is under-worked.

Evidence from a live sample. I pulled the top-1000 models by downloads plus the 500 newest today and analyzed them: downloads run from 240,250,376 down to a ~397k p10 (median 802,470); 518/1000 are Apache-2.0, 185 MIT; tasks are dominated by text-generation (233) and image-text-to-text (157). Supply is corporately concentrated — 319 distinct authors, led by Qwen (117 of the top models), Google (44), Nvidia (37), Microsoft (36), Meta (35). And the 500 newest public models were created inside a ~2.4-hour window → ~205 new public models per hour. Script + breakdowns in analysis/2026-08-12/.

Access & cost. Free JSON API, no auth, huggingface_hub client; rate-limited but bulk-friendly — a weekend to snapshot, a standing cron to build the time series nobody has. Nearest work / difference: prior studies snapshot documentation quality; nobody tracks the adoption-and-governance panel (who wins downloads, how licenses shift, how fast corporate labs crowd the leaderboard). Headline figure: monthly share of top-model downloads by corporate vs. community authors, 2022→2026. Score: 22/25 (a4 b5 c3 d5 e5).

Runner-ups

DSA Transparency Database. Every content-moderation "statement of reasons" filed by 700+ platforms under the EU DSA — ~38M per day, with fields flagging automated detection/decision (download page). The canonical dataset for AI's role in governing information (automated moderation at scale). Honest caveat: not unclaimed — the headline "most decisions are automated" finding is already published on 156M- and 439M-SoR samples (arXiv 2312.04431), so c≈2; our only edge is a narrower unworked cross-section (e.g., automation-rate divergence across languages/countries over time). Score: 22/25 (a5 b5 c2 d5 e5).

X Community Notes public data. Full notes + ratings, daily bulk since Jan 2021 (repo); AI nexus is the bridging-based ranking algorithm plus X's 2025 LLM-written "AI Note Writers." Panel-able by contributor/note; on-frontier platform exhaust. Claimed-ish — a comprehensive 4-year analysis exists (Zenodo 16761304) — so c≈3. Score: 20/25 (a4 b5 c3 d4 e4).
