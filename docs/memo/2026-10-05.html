Top find: AI & algorithmic-management clauses in CBAs (WageIndicator)

The WageIndicator Collective Agreements Database has collected and annotated collective bargaining agreements since 2011; with UNI Europa and the Friedrich-Ebert-Stiftung it now codes the AI and algorithmic-management clauses inside them (UNI Europa). It's the revealed record of what labor actually wins on AI — not essays about it.

AI nexus + agenda. Intrinsic: the coded clauses govern workplace AI. Representation (organized worker voice bargaining over AI) + Governance (private binding rules for AI), cross-national.

Why it clears the bar. CBAs are negotiated outcomes under real stakes — revealed bargaining behavior, what unions actually secured (a4); agreements accrete as a byproduct of bargaining since 2011, the AI-clause layer growing as AI spreads (b4); the broad DB is mined in labor research, but the AI/algorithmic-management-clause slice is new (2024–2026) and so far worked mainly by its own consortium — little independent academic use surfaced (c3); panel-able by agreement × country × sector × clause-type × year (d4); dead-on agenda with an intrinsic AI nexus (e5). Score: 20/25 (a4 b4 c3 d4 e5).

Evidence from pages fetched today. The WageIndicator CBA database now holds more than 3,600 agreements across 76 countries (UNI Europa), with the AI layer coding clauses on data protection, monitoring/surveillance, algorithmic management, human oversight, information/consultation, and training (WageIndicator); the European dashboard is "updated yearly" with clause text examples. The gap it fills is real: a 2024 UNI Europa survey of 148 affiliates across 32 countries found only 20% already have CBAs addressing AI, while 42% are actively negotiating it — bargaining is still ahead of the contracts. Artifacts in analysis/2026-10-05/.

Access & cost. The CBA texts are publicly browsable and the broader database is archived in the CESSDA catalogue; the AI-clause coding surfaces through the dashboard plus downloadable UNI Europa/FES/WageIndicator reports. A clean machine-readable AI-clause CSV likely needs the CESSDA deposit or a WageIndicator data request — the honest build step. A weekend to assemble the agreement × country × clause-type panel. Nearest work / difference: the consortium publishes descriptive reports; nobody has built the open cross-national panel of which protections (human oversight vs. data rights vs. advance notice) diffuse across which countries and sectors, and how fast.
