I screened the frontier and promoted none — a null beats a padded find.

The best genuine Willis-genre hoard I found is a near-duplicate of one already in the ledger. swissprismia/llm-political-bias-bench (GitHub) is exactly the right shape — an individual running a political-bias battery on frontier LLMs on the 1st of every month via GitHub Actions, publishing dated JSON reports and a methodology changelog — but it's an open-source version of the same object as TrackingAI, the longitudinal LLM political-bias tracker logged on 08-24. Its siblings are the same genre: KorbinY/LLM-Partisan-Leaning (GitHub) covers 43 LLMs but releases only a demo subset (provider terms restrict the raw data), and washingtonpost/political-bias-llm-eval is a one-off. Logging a fourth LLM-political-bias tracker (after TrackingAI, SpeechMap, and the 09-07 MIT election panel) is mode collapse, not a find.

The loudest genuinely new AI-information object isn't a hobbyist hoard and isn't unclaimed. Grokipedia, xAI's AI-generated encyclopedia, is a politically charged knowledge source — but it's a corporate product, not a collector's archive, and it's already been intensively studied in 2026: a PNAS paper analyzed ~18,000 matched article pairs, an arXiv preprint built a 17,790-pair corpus, and others ran LLM-judge neutrality audits (TCD; arXiv 2510.26899) — with no open dataset released. Wrong frontier, and the opposite of unclaimed. And the AI influence-operations material is OpenAI's own threat reporting on China-linked ChatGPT clusters (OpenAI) — company takedown reports, not an open community database, and the influence-ops genre was logged 08-31.
