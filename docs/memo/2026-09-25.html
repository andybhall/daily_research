Who the AI industry imports to build its models

The H-1B visa filings for AI and machine-learning roles — how many, at what (very high) wages, and which companies dominate the import of global AI talent — from public U.S. Department of Labor data.

Third pass on frontier 8 (08-26 union AI clauses/NLRB, NYC LL144 bias audits, Worker Info Exchange; 09-15 AI-attributed layoffs). Those cover AI governing, auditing, and displacing workers. The un-mined labor record — and one I flagged as next-to-probe on 09-15 — is the supply side: the visa filings for the people who build AI.

Top find: AI/ML H-1B labor market (DOL OFLC LCA disclosure data)

Every H-1B hire requires a Labor Condition Application, and DOL publishes them: employer, job title, SOC code, offered wage, and worksite, in quarterly bulk files back to 2008 (DOL OFLC). The AI/ML slice is a regulatory-exhaust panel of who imports AI talent, where, and at what price.

AI nexus + agenda. The AI industry's labor supply and the concentration of AI talent in a few firms — the economic edge of the agenda, governance-adjacent (AI-power concentration; the AI-talent immigration fight), which I'll flag honestly rather than overstate.

Why it clears the bar. Real filings under legal/immigration stakes — revealed hiring behavior (a4); LCA disclosures accrue quarterly since 2008, a byproduct of the visa process (b4); partly claimed — H-1B data is heavily mined in general and commercial trackers slice AI/ML, but no academic AI-talent-concentration panel (c3); rich panel by employer × role × wage × worksite × year (d4); AI-relevant but economic, not squarely political (e3). Score: 18/25 (a4 b4 c3 d4 e3).

Evidence from a live sample. Verified this run from OFLC-derived reporting (VisaPulse, sourced to DOL LCA data): FY2025 saw 7,536 AI/ML LCA filings, up from 2,807 in FY2020 — +168%; the average AI/ML wage is $277K vs. $121K across all H-1B — a 131% premium. The talent is concentrated: top sponsors are Amazon (4,252 filings), Apple (2,115), Microsoft (1,989), LinkedIn, and Qualcomm; top titles are Machine Learning Engineer and Applied Scientist; California alone has ~15,000 filings. Artifacts in analysis/2026-09-25/. Honest caveat: I verified the AI-slice aggregate and confirmed the DOL bulk xlsx (FY2008–2025) as the open source, but did not compute the full panel from the raw files this run — that's the build step; some cited sponsor/title counts are cumulative, not FY2025-only.

Access & cost. Free — DOL quarterly LCA .xlsx (large; a few GB across years) plus record-layout PDFs; a weekend to build the employer × AI-role × wage × worksite × year panel by filtering SOC codes and AI/ML titles. Nearest work / difference: commercial trackers report aggregate AI/ML totals; nobody has built the open concentration panel — how the AI-talent market centralized in Big Tech and a few metros, or how wages track each model race.
