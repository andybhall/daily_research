Dataset Hunt — 2026-08-20

Frontier: 2. Money trails (procurement, financial disclosures, Form 990s, lobbying registrations, grants, subawards, sole-source contracts) Verdict: Follow the AI money — federal AI procurement is a live, queryable panel of which vendors the state pays to build its AI, up ~7× since 2018 and DoD-dominated.

The AI-focus gate reframes money trails as three flows: government → AI vendors (procurement), vendors → government (lobbying), funders → advocacy (philanthropy). All three are fresh, on-agenda (Governance/Information political economy of AI), and only lightly worked.

Top find: US federal AI procurement (USASpending)

Every federal contract obligation is public via the free USASpending API; filtering to AI turns it into a vendor-level panel of who the government pays to build and run its AI. Distinct from the ledger's rejected general FPDS panel — this is the AI slice, which only Brookings has begun to analyze (Brookings 2026).

AI nexus + agenda. The money the state spends acquiring AI — bullseye Governance (AI procurement is named in the agenda), AI nexus intrinsic.

Why it clears the bar. Real obligations under real stakes (a); procurement exhaust accruing continuously (b); panel-able by agency × vendor × fiscal year × program (d). Unclaimed (c≈3): general USASpending is heavily mined and Brookings has published AI-spending overviews, but the vendor-competition / concentration panel is unbuilt.

Evidence from a live sample. Querying the API today (keyword "artificial intelligence", contract awards): AI obligations rose $54.6M (FY18) → $209M (FY23) → $386M (FY25) — a ~7× climb. DoD dominates at $857.9M of FY23–26 AI-keyword contracts (next: HHS $65M, Commerce $60M, VA $56M). Top recipients are mid-tier integrators — ECS Federal ($78.5M), MORSECORP ($49.9M), Two Six Labs ($38.9M), Accenture Federal ($38.6M), Scale AI ($27.8M). Honest caveat: keyword-in-description is a conservative slice — huge ceiling vehicles like Palantir's $10B Army agreement aren't AI-keyword-tagged, so Brookings' broader method reports far larger totals ($7.2B obligated FY26). Script + CSVs in analysis/2026-08-20/. Score: 23/25 (a5 b5 c3 d5 e5).

Access & cost. Free JSON API, no auth; a weekend for the keyword panel, a few weeks to add PSC/NAICS codes and ceiling vehicles. Nearest work / difference: Brookings totals the spend; nobody has modeled market concentration, incumbency, or which vendors convert small AI pilots into billion-dollar vehicles. Headline figure: HHI of federal AI-contract dollars by agency, 2018→2026.

Runner-ups

AI lobbying disclosures (LDA). Quarterly Lobbying Disclosure Act filings name who lobbies Congress and the executive on "artificial intelligence," with dollars and specific issues, in the open Senate/House databases (bulk data; LDA overview). Behaviorally-revealed influence spending, panel-able by registrant × issue × quarter; the AI-issue subset (plus new vehicles like the pro-AI super PAC "Leading the Future") is under-worked. Distinct from the FARA seed (this is domestic LDA). Score: 21/25 (a5 b4 c3 d4 e5).

AI-safety / AI-policy philanthropy. Who funds the AI-governance advocacy field — Open Philanthropy (now "Coefficient Giving") publishes a grants database covering AI-risk grants to CAIS, RAND, CDT, CAIDP and others, mirrored by third parties. A money trail into the think-tanks shaping AI rules; panel-able by grantee × year. Score: 19/25 (a4 b4 c3 d4 e4).
