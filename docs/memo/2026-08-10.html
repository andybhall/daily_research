Dataset Hunt — 2026-08-10

Frontier: 2. Money trails (procurement, financial disclosures, Form 990s, lobbying registrations, grants, subawards, sole-source contracts) Verdict: FARA foreign-agent filings — a 1942→2026 panel of who is paid to lobby Washington on behalf of which foreign government, bulk-downloadable and barely mined at the relationship level.

Top find: FARA foreign-agent filings (DOJ eFile)

Every agent of a foreign principal engaged in US political activity must register and file under the 1938 Foreign Agents Registration Act — registrations, semi-annual supplements, short forms, and foreign-principal records, exposed via a public API and a daily bulk repository (DOJ eFile API; FARA user's guide, Hye Young You).

Why it clears the bar. These are payments and lobbying contacts logged under real legal stakes (criminal exposure for non-registration) — revealed foreign-influence behavior, not stated preference (a). Filings accumulate incidentally across decades (b) and are deeply panel-able: the same registrants represent the same foreign principals across years (d). Maps to Information (foreign persuasion of US policy) and Governance (e).

Unclaimed — honestly. Not virgin: a small foreign-lobbying literature exists (You 2020; Grotteria & Miller, "Foreign Influence in US Politics") and OpenSecrets' Foreign Lobby Watch indexes the money. But the relationship- and contact-level data stays under-exploited precisely because the granular exhibits are scattered scanned PDFs — criterion c is the one discount (≈3/5).

Evidence from a live sample. I pulled the DOJ bulk registrant and foreign-principal tables today (via the OpenSanctions us_fara_filings mirror, refreshed 2026-08-10) and analyzed them: 7,075 registrants, 17,724 foreign-principal relationships, registrant dates spanning 1942–2026, with 28% of registrants representing more than one foreign principal (one represents 278) — the panel structure is real, not asserted. Top foreign-principal countries: Japan 6.6%, Canada 4.6%, Mexico 3.3%, Great Britain 3.1%, with USSR (1.7%) showing the historical depth; new foreign-principal registrations rise through the first Trump term (2017: 232 → 2019: 308). Script + full country table in analysis/2026-08-10/.

Access & cost. Bulk CSV/XML daily + JSON API, no auth; the structured registrant/principal tables are a weekend, but linking the narrative activity exhibits (who lobbied whom, on what bill) means OCR over scanned PDFs — a quarter. Nearest work / our difference: existing studies aggregate spend; nobody has built the registrant×foreign-principal×year influence panel to ask whether foreign lobbying reallocates toward the party in power after each election. Headline figure: a country→committee flow of foreign-agent contacts over 20 years. Score: 22/25 (a5 b4 c3 d5 e5).

Runner-ups

India electoral bonds (SBI/ECI disclosure). Court-ordered March-2024 release of the previously-anonymous corporate-to-party donation instrument: 22,217 bonds purchased Apr 2019–Feb 2024, with purchaser (company) and redeemer (party) sides both disclosed (ECI release; granular SBI dataset on Kaggle). Behaviorally revealed corporate money under (broken) anonymity, panel-able by company and party, freshly disclosed and academically near-untouched beyond ADR/journalism. Representation + Governance. Note: the aggregate Dataful mirror (894 rows) lacks donor→party linkage — the granular ECI/SBI tables are the prize. Score: 22/25 (a5 b4 c4 d4 e5).

GAO bid-protest decisions. Firms formally contesting who wins federal contracts — GAO resolves 1,000+ protests a year, issuing 380 merits decisions in FY25 (GAO search; GAO-25-108652). Revealed behavior by losing bidders under real money stakes, panel-able by agency and repeat protester, and empirically under-mined. Governance. Access is a text/PDF corpus (GAO site + third-party Tango API); not probed today to keep the pull bounded. Score: 21/25 (a4 b4 c4 d4 e5).
