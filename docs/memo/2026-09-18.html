Top find: In re ChatGPT Product Liability Cases (JCCP 5431)

On 2026-02-03 the California Superior Court coordinated the wave of suits blaming ChatGPT for real-world harm into one proceeding, JCCP 5431 (Judge Schulman, San Francisco Dept 304; docket ref CJC25005431). It is the AI equivalent of an MDL — a single, tracked mass tort at the frontier of AI product-liability law.

AI nexus + agenda. Bullseye Governance — courts deciding whether and how an AI product is liable for what it says to users. AI nexus intrinsic; distinct from DAIL's case index (09-08) — this is the coordinated proceeding with shared discovery and a defined docket.

Why it clears the bar. Real wrongful-death and injury suits under the highest stakes — revealed behavior, not opinion (a5); the coordinated docket accretes case-by-case and will run for years (b3); academically unclaimed — heavy law-firm and press coverage, but no scholarly dataset of the proceeding (c3); panel-able by case × injury-type × county × outcome over time (d3, growing); dead-on Governance (e5). Score: 19/25 (a5 b3 c3 d3 e5).

Evidence from a live sample. Verified this run from the proceeding's status filings and coordination order: 23 cases coordinated as of 2026-08-25 — 12 wrongful-death/suicide (including one murder-suicide) and 11 personal-injury / severe psychological-harm cases — across four California counties (San Francisco, Los Angeles, Alameda, San Diego). Defendants are OpenAI, related entities, and Samuel Altman (Mayer Brown), plus Microsoft in one case. Case Management Order No. 1 (2026-08-04) named four co-lead counsel and a five-member steering committee; next conference 2026-09-23. Facts + sources in analysis/2026-09-18/. (The per-case docket lives in the SF Superior Court system; no bulk API, so the panel is built case-by-case.)

Access & cost. SF Superior Court case search (ref CJC25005431) + the coordinated cases' individual dockets; a few weeks to assemble a case × injury-category × allegation × ruling panel as the JCCP proceeds. Nearest work / difference: trackers list the cases; nobody has structured the proceeding — which product features (memory, sycophancy, safety-filter failures) recur across complaints, and how courts rule on the novel product-liability-of-speech questions. Headline figure: coordinated cases by injury type over time, against OpenAI's safety-policy changes.

Runner-up: AI-litigation discovery documents (NYT / Authors Guild v OpenAI)

The copyright cases (In re OpenAI, 1:25-md-03143 SDNY) are disgorging internal AI-company records — the "discovery exhibits" corner. Surfaced so far (TechCrunch): a ~78M de-identified ChatGPT-conversation database OpenAI built to evaluate copyright regurgitation; a "Project Giraffe" Bloom filter logging regurgitation; internal training-corpus searches contradicting claimed technical inability; and the destroyed Books1/Books2 datasets. It's the Opioid-Archive genre for AI — primary internal documents on how models were actually built. Caveat: much is sealed/redacted (a 20M-log sample was filed but plaintiffs call it "unusable"), so there's no open archive yet — the object is the unsealing trail. Score: 18/25 (a5 b3 c3 d2 e5).
