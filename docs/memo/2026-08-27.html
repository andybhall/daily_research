Dataset Hunt — 2026-08-27

Frontier: 9. Markets & governance (prediction markets, DAO votes, proxy voting, shareholder resolutions, resolution disputes) Verdict: Corporate America now names AI a material risk in ~3,300 annual reports a year — an EDGAR-queryable panel of how firms govern and disclose AI, up ~8× since 2019.

Revisiting frontier 9 (08-17 took AI shareholder resolutions, Manifold, Metaculus). Today's distinct AI angles: firms' own AI-risk disclosures, and the newest twist — AI agents becoming autonomous participants in markets and governance.

Top find: Corporate AI-risk disclosures in 10-K filings (EDGAR)

Every US public company files an annual 10-K with a risk-factor section; a rapidly-growing share now names AI as a material risk. The filings are fully queryable via the free EDGAR full-text-search API, turning them into a company × year × risk-type panel of how firms govern and frame AI. Distinct from the 08-17 shareholder-resolution find (that's investor proposals; this is management's own disclosures).

AI nexus + agenda. How firms disclose and govern their own AI exposure — Governance (corporate governance of AI), AI nexus intrinsic.

Why it clears the bar. Mandatory disclosures under SEC/legal stakes, not opinion (a); risk-factor exhaust accruing every fiscal year (b); panel-able by company × year × risk-type (d); on-agenda (e5). Unclaimed — honestly (c≈2): this is now a hot research area — arXiv 2508.19313, Harvard corpgov, a dedicated AI Disclosures Project, and a released firm-year Mendeley panel (~50–60 firms). The unmined edge is the full 7,000-firm EDGAR panel and the disclosure gaps (risk types firms systematically omit — environmental, labor displacement, info-ecosystem pollution).

Evidence from a live sample. Querying EDGAR today (10-K, "artificial intelligence"): 445 (2019) → 848 (2021) → 1,295 (2023) → 2,436 (2024) → 3,324 (2025) → 3,754 (2026) — an ~8× rise; and 3,242 of the 2025 filings name AI in a risk-factor context, so AI has moved from novelty to standard material risk. Corroborated: 380 S&P 500 firms (76%) expanded AI-risk language in 2025. Script + CSV in analysis/2026-08-27/. Score: 22/25 (a5 b5 c2 d5 e5).

Access & cost. Free API + bulk 10-K text; a weekend for the mention panel, a few weeks to classify risk types and link to outcomes. Nearest work / difference: studies count mentions on ~50 firms; nobody has built the full-universe panel or modeled which AI risks firms hide vs. hype by industry. Headline figure: share of 10-Ks framing AI as risk vs. opportunity, by sector, 2019→2026.
