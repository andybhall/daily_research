Top find: Voter Prompts — real voters' AI conversations about the 2026 midterms

Yale SOM researchers (Beth Anne Helgason, Maddison Linker, Rishi Gurudevan) ran two preregistered studies (AsPredicted #317878, #319077) on 16–27 Oct 2026 across the 35 states with a 2026 Senate race, and released the chats (voter-prompts; methods; Hugging Face bahelgason/voter-prompts). Participants used ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or Grok inside the survey, as they would on their own, to decide how to vote in their Senate race.

AI nexus + agenda. Bullseye Representation + Information — how citizens actually use AI to make a voting decision, and whether the models recommend candidates. The demand side, distinct from the supply-side MIT LLM Election Observatory (09-07) and the Grok/Perplexity fact-check requests (09-17).

Why it clears the bar. Revealed behavior under real election stakes — real voters' own prompts as they decided (a4); but survey-elicited and point-in-time, not incidental exhaust (b2); brand-new, released explicitly "to help eval builders," no outside analysis yet (c4); panel-able by chat × model × voter × turn × code × race (d3); dead-on agenda with an intrinsic AI nexus (e5). Score: 18/25 (a4 b2 c4 d3 e5).

Evidence (fetched this run). 2,430 participants, ~2,268 coded chats (Study 1 all-voters: 1,455/1,372; Study 2 undecided: 975/896); models GPT-5.6, Claude Sonnet 5.5, Gemini 3.8 Flash, and Grok 4.3; chats ran via OpenRouter, each user message coded 3× by Claude Opus 5.5 against a codebook. 61% of users asked basic ballot facts; 36% asked for help choosing. Unprompted, only Grok regularly named a candidate (14%, 8/59; ChatGPT 2/74; Claude and Gemini never). After a user shared their views, candidate recommendation diverged sharply — ChatGPT 79%, Grok 66%, Gemini 53%, Claude 40% — and after a user answered the bot's priority question, ChatGPT recommended in 88% (21/24) vs Claude 7% (1/15). Chats were short (median 2 messages). Artifacts in analysis/2026-11-06/.

Access & cost. Released on Hugging Face (chats with identifying details removed + codebook + coding outputs); a public chat-explorer page; participants consented to release, PII placeholdered before any API call. Free; a weekend to analyze model × recommendation-behavior × voter-identity. Nearest work / difference: the MIT Observatory audits what models say to simulated users; this captures what real voters ask and how models steer them. Honest caveats: survey-elicited, not in-the-wild exhaust (the authors note only AI companies hold the full real transcripts, and urge release); modest N (~2,268); Hugging Face license not stated on the page.
