Millions of internal corporate records released in opioid litigation, openly downloadable and barely analyzed.

Top find: Opioid Industry Documents Archive (OIDA)

Millions of previously-internal documents — "company emails, memos, presentations, sales reports, budgets, audit reports, Drug Enforcement Administration briefings, meeting agendas and minutes, expert witness reports, and depositions of company executives" — made public through opioid litigation settlements and judgments, curated by UCSF + Johns Hopkins (UCSF launch). Individual releases are already huge: 1.4M documents from Mallinckrodt and 114k from McKinsey (JHU).

Why it clears the bar. This is discovery exhaust in its purest form: business records created incidentally under real commercial stakes, then disgorged by courts — revealed corporate conduct, not stated preference (criteria a, b). It is panel-able by firm (Purdue, Mallinckrodt, McKinsey, distributors) across years, and maps to Governance (corporate conduct + regulatory capture of the DEA) and Information (corporate political strategy) — criterion e.

Unclaimed — honestly. Not virgin: a Social Science & Medicine 2023 piece sketches "new directions in research on corporate political strategy" (ScienceDirect) and an AAAI 2026 paper built an AI benchmark, OIDA-QA, on 400k docs (arXiv). But that's a methods benchmark and a directions essay; the corpus of millions remains almost entirely unexploited as a quantitative firm×year influence panel — criterion c is the one discount (≈3/5).

Access & cost. Bulk-open on the AWS Registry of Open Data — metadata, source files (PDF/Excel/PPT) and OCR text in a public S3 bucket, no account needed (aws s3 ls --no-sign-request s3://opioid-industry-documents-archive-dataset-bucket/), updated monthly (AWS). Collection is already done; the real cost is document classification + storage — a subset (McKinsey's 114k) is a weekend, the full archive a quarter. Nearest work / our difference: existing work is qualitative or an ML benchmark; nobody has built a firm×year panel of influence events (regulator contacts, sales-quota escalations, consultant playbooks). Headline figure: per-firm timeline of documented regulator-contact and sales-pressure events overlaid on shipment and overdose-death curves. First analysis: within-firm event study — after a DEA enforcement threat, does documented lobbying/consulting activity spike? Identified off enforcement-action timing.

Score: 22/25 (a5 b5 c3 d4 e5).

Runner-up: DataJud — Brazil's National Judiciary Database (CNJ public API)

Procedural metadata and docket movements for cases across every instance of the Brazilian judiciary (STJ, TST, TSE, STM, all state/regional courts), systematically collected since 2009, with >98.9% of new cases filed electronically, exposed through a public Elasticsearch-backed API (CNJ API wiki; ACM ICEGOV). Bureaucratic exhaust at national scale: case classes, subjects, and movement histories piling up (criteria a4, b5). Panel-able by court/case-type/month; Governance layer. Prior use is real but mostly administrative-transparency and Portuguese-language (DGO2025); it is under-exploited as a behavioral substrate — litigant strategy, delay, appeal cascades (c≈3). Terms of use explicitly permit academic research. First analysis: staggered-DiD on electronic-filing/PJe rollout across courts → disposition time. Score: 21/25 (a4 b5 c3 d4 e5).
