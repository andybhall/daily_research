What AI chatbots refuse to say (SpeechMap)

795,000+ logged chatbot responses to controversial prompts, showing how AI models handle political speech over time.

August 24, 2026
22/25
Willis score

The AI-focus gate finds its purest frontier-6 fit here: independent individuals hoarding structured data on how AI systems handle politics — speech refusals, hidden configs, political bias. Three fresh finds, all Governance/Information, none in the ledger (distinct from the 08-14 L1B3RT4S/Civitai/Pixiv finds).

Top find: SpeechMap.ai

An independent project (by "xlr8harder") that puts the same battery of controversial requests — political argument, satire, religion, history, rights advocacy — to hundreds of models and records whether each answers, hedges, or refuses (speechmap.ai).

AI nexus + agenda. A direct measure of how AI systems govern political expression — bullseye Governance/Information (AI as gatekeeper of political speech), AI nexus intrinsic.

Why it clears the bar. Real model refusal/compliance behavior, not stated policy (a); evaluations accreting continuously as models ship (b); a genuine panel of model × topic × time (d); on-agenda (e5). Unclaimed (c≈4): cited by Meta's Oversight Board and covered in tech press, but academic exploitation as a longitudinal free-speech panel is thin.

Evidence from a live sample. The site reports 375 models and 795,000 responses analyzed, March 2023 → August 2026, with four outcome codes (Complete / Evasive / Denial / Error). Per-topic asymmetries are already visible: "argue for traditional gender roles" draws 61% compliance vs 92.6% with genders reversed; "outlaw Judaism" 10.5% vs "outlaw witchcraft" 68.5%. Partial probe only — the site exposes structured per-model/per-topic results, but bulk data needs a direct request (no public API); logged in analysis/2026-08-24/. Score: 22/25 (a4 b4 c4 d5 e5).

Access & cost. Browsable now; a clean panel needs the maintainer's data or a scrape — a weekend to a couple of weeks. Nearest work / difference: press covers snapshots; nobody has modeled how each lab's refusal frontier moves across model versions and political topics over three years. Headline figure: per-lab compliance rate on political speech, by topic, 2023→2026.

Runner-ups

Leaked system-prompt archives. A fan-curated hoard of the hidden system prompts configuring frontier AI (leaked-system-prompts.com; GitHub YeeKal, sibling CL4R1T4S). Cloned and analyzed today (metadata only): 116 prompt files across 20+ vendors — Anthropic (24), OpenAI (18), xAI (13), Perplexity/Google (6) — with a version panel by year (2022: 3 → 2024: 36 → 2025: 49 → 2026: 10). The hidden "constitutions" that encode each model's behavioral and political guardrails; distinct from the 08-14 L1B3RT4S jailbreak find (this is the config side). Governance. Score: 21/25 (a4 b4 c4 d4 e5).

TrackingAI.org. Maxim Lott's independent tracker re-runs the Political Compass (and IQ tests) against ~19+ LLMs as they update, charting political-bias drift over time (trackingai.org). A hobbyist longitudinal panel of AI political orientation. Honest caveat: the underlying idea is David Rozado's published LLM-political-preference work, so c is lower. Score: 20/25 (a4 b4 c3 d4 e5).
