Whether agencies actually follow the federal AI rules

GAO's audit recommendations pushing federal agencies to comply with AI-management requirements — which agencies got flagged, for what, and whether they've fixed it — tracked open-vs-closed over time.

Those are the systems, the laws, and the legislature. The un-mined institutional record is oversight — the auditor checking the government's own AI homework.

Top find: GAO federal-AI oversight recommendations

The Government Accountability Office audits federal AI and issues formal, numbered recommendations to agencies, then tracks each one's implementation status (Open / Closed-Implemented / Closed-Not Implemented) in its public Recommendations Database. It is a living accountability record: who was told to fix what, and whether they did.

AI nexus + agenda. Bullseye Governance — independent oversight of how the federal government builds and manages AI. AI nexus intrinsic; distinct from the Federal AI Use Case Inventory (the systems, 08-13), the de-published CAIO roster (staffing), and the Congressional AI hearings (legislative, 09-02).

Why it clears the bar. Real audit findings with binding agency responses under accountability stakes (a4); GAO's AI recommendations accrue continuously — ~50 AI products since 2018, 20 ongoing (b3); unclaimed as a panel — GAO reports are widely cited individually, but the status-tracked AI-recommendation panel is unmined academically (c3); panel-able by recommendation × agency × status × year (d3, modest N); dead-on Governance (e5). Score: 18/25 (a4 b3 c3 d3 e5).

Evidence from a live sample. I pulled GAO's flagship AI report (GAO-24-105980) and the AI-work overview (GAO-24-107237) this run. The flagship made 35 recommendations to 19 agencies; their statuses today: 17 Closed-Implemented, 15 Open, 3 Closed-Not-Implemented. OPM drew the most (5). The gaps are concrete: only 5 of 23 agencies had comprehensive AI use-case inventories (15 were incomplete/inaccurate), OMB had not issued AI acquisition guidance, and OPM had not created an AI occupational series. Artifacts in analysis/2026-09-22/.

Access & cost. Free — the GAO Recommendations Database filters by topic and exposes each recommendation's status; ~50 AI reports give the fuller corpus. A weekend to assemble the recommendation × agency × status × year panel and diff it against the agencies' own AI inventories. Nearest work / difference: GAO's own reports summarize each audit; nobody has built the cross-report panel or tested which agencies systematically stall AI oversight, or whether "Closed-Implemented" tracks real inventory improvements.
