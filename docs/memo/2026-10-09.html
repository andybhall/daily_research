Three-and-a-half weeks before the midterms the AI-political-money story is louder than ever — but louder is not the same as unclaimed. Today's candidates fail the one check this frontier keeps failing: the data is maximally mined, the AI slice is already built, and the object is the other-half of a find already in the ledger. I screened and promoted none — a null beats a padded find.

The headline development is real and on-agenda: the AI-regulation fight now has two opposing super PACs. Against the acceleration-side Leading the Future (logged 09-09), the safety side has stood up Public First Action, substantially funded by Anthropic ($20M committed) with a $1M personal gift from Dario Amodei (Washington Examiner; Transformer). And FEC itemized data reveals a sharp intra-industry donor cleavage — OpenAI employees gave ~$296K this cycle almost entirely to Democrats, while Anthropic staff gave ~$1.6M in Q2 (≈69% Democratic), at far higher participation rates (SF Standard). Public Citizen puts crypto+AI+Big-Tech+betting super-PAC spending near $500M for the cycle (Public Citizen).

But none of this is a hunt find. The AI-industry employee/executive giving object is built from FEC individual itemized contributions — the single most-mined dataset in American campaign finance — and the AI-employer slice specifically has already been pulled and published this cycle by multiple outlets (SF Standard; Transformer), so it fails "unclaimed" at both the genre and slice level; its AI nexus is also only via employer. Public First Action is a genuinely distinct FEC committee, but it's the same object-type as the Leading the Future find (an AI super PAC's filings) — the other side of a money war I already logged on 09-09, an update rather than a new object, and committee-level FEC data is well-mined. Logging either would be padding the most-covered AI-money story of the cycle. (Q3 FEC reports, due mid-October, weren't available this run — but a refresh of logged committees wouldn't change the verdict.)
