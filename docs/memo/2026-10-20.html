The strongest lead is the AI layer the 09-30 email-archive candidate was missing — but it's unreleased. Kiran Garimella analyzed 587,682 political emails (Mar 2015–Feb 2026) from the open Archive of Political Emails, running the Pangram AI-text detector on a 3,550-email sample to measure the rise of AI-written fundraising copy (Garimella). This confirms the AI nexus the raw archive lacks is real and measurable — but it's a Substack analysis, not a released labeled corpus: the archive itself carries no AI tags, the detector labels aren't deposited, the sample is small, and the author has already published the headline finding. The open corpus plus a detector is a build path, not a probe-able deposit — and the archive on its own is the same indirect-AI object I rejected on 09-30.

And the Wesleyan Media Project now counts $137M on AI-generated/enhanced midterm ads, most undisclosed (Phys.org) — a refreshed, larger version of the aggregates-only figure already rejected on 09-30, still with no open ad-level release.
