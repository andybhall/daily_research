World Bank database of government AI purchases worldwide

17,685 government contracts to buy AI systems, drawn from EU, World Bank and Open Contracting tender records (2003–2025) and tagged by what the AI does — from predictive analytics to biometric surveillance.

Top find: World Bank "AI Procurement Contracts" (dataset 0067129)

A CC-BY Excel the World Bank quietly published in July 2026 as supporting data for the World Development Report 2026: The Promise of Artificial Intelligence: the AI-related subset of ~6.1M government procurement notices its team classified with an LLM, pulled from three real tender streams — the EU's TED, the World Bank's own contracts, and the Open Contracting Partnership (catalog; reproducibility pkg). Each row is one government's contract to buy an AI system, with the tender title plus classification fields (task_result, tool_category, autonomy_result, income group). This is procurement exhaust — revealed spending under budget and political stakes, piling up over two decades — not a survey or a constructed benchmark.

Why it's unclaimed and worth taking: the row-level panel has been used for exactly two aggregate figures (WDR-2026 Fig 5.2/5.7) and nothing else. A real-equivalent Scholar/web sweep this run found no scholarly cross-country AI-procurement panel exists — one AI-index report names "the absence of a single global dataset for AI procurement volumes" as a field-wide gap (Alice Labs). The nearest scholarly object is Fazekas et al.'s Global Public Procurement Dataset (72M contracts, 42 countries, 2006–2021) — general procurement with no AI tagging (livinglib). Our object is the AI slice plus a what-does-the-AI-do taxonomy the GPPD lacks. (The gemmozero/ai-procurement-2026 HF table that surfaces alongside is the unreliable vendor set — post-dated Palantir/OpenAI rows, no WB link — and is ignored.)

Access is a free 3MB .xlsx download + a DDH API (file); collection cost is a weekend, not a quarter. Headline figure nobody has drawn: the global composition of state AI buying by function — and in particular how much of it is surveillance/biometric. First analysis: do governments with weaker democratic institutions procure disproportionately more Surveillance & Biometric AI? Identification: within-country over time (23-year notice dates) against income group / a democracy index, using the 102 countries with ≥20 contracts.

Evidence from a live sample: I pulled the full file this run and parsed it with a stdlib xlsx reader. 17,685 AI contracts, notice dates 2003→2025; sources Open Contracting 10,211 / EU TED 4,866 / World Bank 2,608; 1,342 country/region codes, 102 countries with ≥20 contracts (max 1,665 = Netherlands). Income group: High 14,428 (82%), Upper-mid 1,437, Lower-mid 1,391, Low 429. Top functions: Predictive Analytics 5,512, Remote Sensing 2,099, Environmental Monitoring 1,604, Speech/Document 1,236, Surveillance & Biometric 1,159, AI Infrastructure 1,081. One fact you can't get from the landing page: the released model field is deepseek for 100% of rows, though the catalog page credits GPT-5 — a provenance flag worth carrying. Artifact: analysis/2026-11-18/.
