Top find: "@Grok is this true?" — LLM fact-checking requests on X

A working paper by Renault, Rand & Mosleh (OSF psyarxiv/85quw) assembles every English-language post that tagged Grok or Perplexity to fact-check a claim on X. It is the first large-scale look at AI as an organic, user-summoned fact-checker — the moment "ask the chatbot" replaced "check the source."

AI nexus + agenda. Bullseye Information — AI as the public arbiter of political truth, at population scale, summoned by ordinary users. AI nexus intrinsic.

Why it clears the bar. Real users publicly asking AI to settle contested claims in the live info ecosystem — revealed behavior, not survey (a5); organic and continuously accreting since Grok's X integration (b4); emerging-but-not-virgin — this paper and a companion ("Asking Grok," arXiv 2605.19720) work it, but the raw request stream supports far more than they asked (c3); panel-able by request × time × topic × model × asker-partisanship (d4); dead-on Information (e5). Score: 21/25 (a5 b4 c3 d4 e5).

Evidence (verified this run; could not pull raw records — see caveat). Per the Indicator writeup and the paper: 447,083 posts tagged Grok for a fact-check (Mar–Sep 2025), part of ~1.4–1.7 million requests to Grok and Perplexity (Feb–Sep 2025) — 7.6% of all interactions with the bots, and the single most common message to Grok after its X integration. The paper reports a partisan split — Grok fact-check requesters skew Republican, Perplexity's skew Democratic — and that on a 100-post sample the bots agreed with professional fact-checkers ~54.5% (Grok) / 57.7% (Perplexity) of the time. Notably, Grok's launch coincided with a statistically significant decline in Community Notes activity, while AI-authored Community Notes rose ~11%→18%. Caveat / could not probe: the underlying requests are X data, not openly redistributable, so I verified the paper's figures rather than pulling records; the OSF item is the preprint, not an open dataset.
