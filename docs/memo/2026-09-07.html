How AI chatbots answered 2024 election questions, day by day

A near-daily record of how 8 major AI models answered ~600 US-election questions across the 2024 season — the same questions asked over and over, from July to just past Election Day.

Top find: LLM Election Data 2024 (Cen et al., MIT)

An MIT team (Cen, Ilyas, Driss, Park, Hopkins, Podimata, Madry; CSAIL writeup) queried production chatbots with a fixed battery of election questions on a near-daily cadence through the 2024 season and released the raw run at huggingface.co/datasets/sarahcen/llm-election-data-2024 (MIT license). It is the thing the election-audit one-shots in our ledger (StatesUnited, CDT) are not: a longitudinal panel of model behavior, not a snapshot.

AI nexus + agenda. Bullseye Information — how deployed AI answered the exact voting, candidate, and election-integrity questions real users would ask, and how those answers drifted over an election. AI nexus intrinsic.

Why it clears the bar. Revealed behavior of production models under election stakes (a4); genuinely longitudinal, 112 near-daily snapshots per question (b3 — assembled, not incidental); unclaimed — a Google Scholar/web check surfaces only the authors' own paper plus press, no third-party reuse (c4); a textbook panel of model × question × day × partisan-framing (d5); dead-on agenda (e5). Score: 21/25 (a4 b3 c4 d5 e5).

Evidence from a live sample. I pulled the full 92,000-file manifest and three sample question series via the HF API this run. Real numbers: 8 model configurations (claude-3-5-sonnet, -haiku, -opus, sonnet-with-internet, gemini-1.0-pro ±internet, gpt-3.5-turbo, gpt-4-turbo), each with 12,638 question-runs (91,998 CSVs total); 605 distinct questions across three conditions — baseline capability controls, endo voter-facing (election process, issues, candidates), and exo (predictions, exit polls, integrity). Each daily question holds 112 near-daily snapshots, 2024-07-23 → 2024-11-12, in 19 fields including a partisan pre-prompt ("I am a Republican. {}") and placeholder variations ({'state': 'South Carolina'}) for steering tests. Artifacts + sample series in analysis/2026-09-07/.

Access & cost. Public HF repo, no auth; ~92k small CSVs, a few GB — a huggingface-hub snapshot and a weekend to reshape into a model×question×day panel. Nearest work / difference: the authors' own paper analyzes steering and implicit predictions; nobody else has touched the deposit, and the obvious external angles — cross-model convergence on contested claims, drift around debates and Election Day, partisan-framing gaps — are wide open. Headline figure: for a fixed election-integrity question, the share of the 8 models giving a "safe/accurate" answer, plotted daily July→November with debate and Election-Day markers.

Watch: paper says "will be publicly available"; no DOI yet, so unverifiable today. Revisit on release.

  • Microsoft Copilot election answers (Zenodo 10517697) | ~14/25 | largest public DB of RAG chatbot responses to voter prompts (Swiss/Bavarian/Hesse 2023) — but a constructed audit and already worked by AlgorithmWatch.
  • LLM-generated parliamentary text detection (Zenodo 20611280) | reject | human vs. Gemini/GPT parliamentary text — a constructed detection benchmark, our OpenFake-genre kill.
  • AI in Everyday Life 2026 (Zenodo 22091497) | reject | course survey of AI attitudes/literacy — stated preference, assembled to be studied.
