Companies blaming AI for their layoffs

A running record of employers that have publicly tied job cuts to AI or automation — who, when, how many, and in which industries — compiled from CEO memos, SEC filings, and earnings calls.

Second pass on frontier 8 (08-26 took Union AI provisions/NLRB, NYC LL144 bias audits, Worker Info Exchange). Those covered AI governing workers and unions bargaining over it. The un-mined object is AI displacing them — and, uniquely in 2026, employers saying so themselves.

Top find: AI-attributed layoffs (corporate self-disclosures)

Since 2023, Challenger, Gray & Christmas has tracked "AI" as a stated reason for US job cuts, and event-level trackers (DisplaceIndex, Founder Reports) log each company that ties cuts to AI in a CEO memo, SEC filing, or earnings call. The self-attribution is the exhaust: firms publicly blaming AI for cutting workers, under investor and legal stakes.

AI nexus + agenda. The political economy of AI at work — displacement is the live AI-labor policy fight. AI nexus intrinsic; maps to Governance/Representation (how AI reshapes work and the policy response).

Why it clears the bar. Real layoffs, self-attributed to AI on the record — revealed behavior, not survey (a5); the disclosures accrue continuously (Challenger monthly since 2023; trackers refresh every 6h) (b4); unclaimed — heavy journalism but no academic company-level panel of AI-attributed cuts (c4); panel-able by company × month × sector (d3, modest N); central to AI politics (e4). Score: 20/25 (a5 b4 c4 d3 e4).

Evidence from a live sample. Verified off the trackers this run: DisplaceIndex logs 377,084 AI-attributed job cuts across 57 companies (Sept 2023→2026); Challenger counted 101,743 US cuts citing AI through June 2026 — ~2× the 54,836 in all of 2025 — with AI the #1 stated layoff reason for four straight months (Mar–Jun 2026). I extracted DisplaceIndex's 47 company-events: by sector, Technology 22, Finance 9, then logistics/education/creative (4 each); by year the event count runs 4 → 13 → 16 → 14 (2023→2026 YTD); named firms include Microsoft, Meta, Salesforce, IBM, Oracle, PayPal, Klarna. Honest caveat: the trackers are web-only and their per-event job numbers parsed unreliably, so I report event counts I computed plus the trackers' own stated totals — not parsed magnitudes. Details in analysis/2026-09-15/.

Access & cost. The clean build isn't the trackers (web-only) but their sources: SEC 8-Ks and earnings-call transcripts (full-text-searchable), WARN notices, and CEO statements — a few weeks to assemble a verified company × date × count × source panel. Nearest work / difference: Challenger reports monthly aggregates; nobody has built the open company-level panel or tested whether "AI" attributions track actual automation vs. cover for cyclical cuts. Headline figure: monthly AI-attributed cuts by sector, 2023→2026, against AI-capex announcements.
