Who shows up to shape U.S. AI rules

The public comments filed on federal AI rulemakings — pulled live from the open regulations.gov API — a record of who contests how AI gets governed, and the very channel the GAO says AI-generated mass comments are now distorting.

October 2, 2026
20/25

Fourth pass on frontier 5 (08-13 Federal AI Use Case Inventory + NL/UK registers; 09-02 NCSL bills, Federal Register rulemaking, Congressional hearings; 09-22 GAO oversight). Those are the systems, the rules, and the auditor. The un-mined institutional record — and one literally named in the frontier — is public comments: the public's side of the rulemaking, not the government's.

Top find: public comments on federal AI rulemakings (regulations.gov)

Every federal AI rule and inquiry opens a notice-and-comment docket, and regulations.gov posts every submission with a documented bulk API. The AI dockets are a byproduct panel of who fights over AI governance — firms, trade groups, artists, agencies — and, increasingly, of bots.

AI nexus + agenda. Intrinsic: the comments are about governing AI. Bullseye Governance (contesting the rules that will bind AI) and Representation (notice-and-comment as a participation channel — now distorted by AI-generated mass comments).

Why it clears the bar. Revealed positions filed under real stakes by organized interests (a4); comments accrue incidentally as a rulemaking byproduct across 2023→2026 dockets (b4); the genre (comments-as-data) is well-mined, but the AI-specific cross-docket participation panel is unbuilt — no corpus analysis of who shapes AI rules surfaced (c3); panel-able by docket × commenter × organization × position × date (d4); dead-on agenda with an intrinsic AI nexus (e5). Score: 20/25 (a4 b4 c3 d4 e5).

Evidence from a live probe. I queried the regulations.gov API this run: the U.S. Copyright Office AI Notice of Inquiry (docket COLC-2023-0006) holds 10,371 comments (meta.totalElements, DEMO_KEY) — the training-data/copyright fight that drew artists and creators en masse (copyright.gov/ai; 34 questions, 10,000+ responses, some >100 pages). By contrast the technical governance inquiries drew mostly organized interests: the NIST AI EO RFI got 214 (nist.gov) and the OMB draft AI memo M-24-10 got 196 (whitehouse.gov). The distortion is now official: a GAO report (July 21, 2026) found the IRS flooded with tens of thousands of comments, some AI-generated, and that "IRS has not developed policies for addressing mass public comments or comments written with the assistance of AI"; FCC staff reportedly reviewed AI-generated summaries of comments rather than the comments themselves (Nextgov; CFO Brew). For scale, the NY AG flagged 18M+ non-authentic comments in the 2017 net-neutrality docket (GW Reg Studies). Artifacts in analysis/2026-10-02/.

Access & cost. Free; a keyed regulations.gov API lifts the DEMO_KEY rate limit for a full pull (comment text, organization, date, attachments) across every AI docket. A weekend to build the docket × commenter × position panel and layer an AI-generated-comment classifier on top. Nearest work / difference: comments-as-data is established (Balla on mass-comment campaigns; influence-tracing work), but nobody has assembled the cross-docket AI-governance participation panel or measured how AI-generated submissions are reshaping it.
