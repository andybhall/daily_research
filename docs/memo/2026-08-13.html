Dataset Hunt — 2026-08-13

Frontier: 5. Institutional records (hearing transcripts, public comments, agendas, visitor logs, staffing/payroll, calendars) Verdict: Government AI-transparency registers — the mandated institutional record of which algorithms the state runs on citizens, a US/NL/UK panel almost nobody has mined longitudinally.

The AI-focus gate turns "institutional records" into a specific, rich seam: governments are now required to disclose their own AI. Three national registers surfaced, all on-frontier and intrinsically AI-relevant (Governance — algorithmic decision-making in institutions).

Top find: US Federal Agency AI Use Case Inventory

Every federal agency must annually inventory its AI systems and post a machine-readable CSV, consolidated by OMB under EO 13960 + M-24-10 (2024 repo; 2025 repo). Each record is one deployed/planned AI system with ~60 fields: agency, purpose, dev stage, impact_type (rights/safety-impacting), commercial-vs-custom, PII, ATO status, appeal process.

AI nexus + agenda. It is the register of the state's own algorithms — bullseye Governance (algorithmic decision-making in institutions), AI nexus intrinsic.

Why it clears the bar. Mandated self-reports that nonetheless reveal real deployments under compliance stakes (a); a byproduct of the annual compliance cycle, accumulating year over year (b); panel-able by agency × year × dev-stage (d). Unclaimed (c≈4): only policy commentary so far (Brookings; an ICTWorks analysis); no rigorous longitudinal academic panel.

Evidence from a live sample. I pulled the 2024 consolidated CSV today and analyzed it: 1,757 use cases across 37 agencies, 62 columns; led by HHS (271), Veterans Affairs (229), Homeland Security (183), Interior (180), USAID (137). 227 systems are rights- or safety-impacting (59 rights + 18 safety + 150 both), and 133 are already Retired — a full lifecycle (627 in Operation & Maintenance, 329 merely Initiated). OMB's 2025 inventory already reports 3,611 use cases across 56 agencies (a 105% YoY jump), so the panel is real. Caveat found in the data: date_initiated is almost entirely blank. Script + breakdowns in analysis/2026-08-13/. Score: 22/25 (a4 b4 c4 d5 e5).

Runner-ups

Netherlands Algorithm Register. A public register of impactful government algorithms (incl. high-risk AI) — 1,533 algorithms as of today, contributed by ministries, provinces, and municipalities, on an open-source platform (algoritmes.overheid.nl; code at MinBZK/Algoritmeregister). Each record documents purpose, data, and the algorithm's role in decisions. Panel-able by body over time; prior academic work audits single algorithms (arXiv 2502.01713), not the register as a corpus. Governance. Score: 21/25 (a4 b4 c4 d4 e5).

UK Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard (ATRS). Structured records of public-sector algorithmic tools, mandatory across government since 2024, published on GOV.UK (hub). ~125 records and growing, each detailing tool purpose, model, data, and oversight. The third leg of a US/NL/UK comparative panel of algorithmic government. Smaller today, so d is lower. Score: 20/25 (a4 b4 c4 d3 e5).
