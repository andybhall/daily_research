Null day — nothing cleared the bar. The screened candidates are recorded below and in the ledger.

Ninth pass on frontier 1 (08-09 seeds; 08-19 AI Hallucination Cases DB; 09-08 Algorithmic Contestability DB / DAIL / BIPA; 09-18 ChatGPT JCCP + AI-discovery docs; 09-28, 10-08, 10-28, 11-17 null). Fifth consecutive null. The last visit was only 10 days ago, so I dug a fresh sub-territory — cross-national coded case databases, amicus/consent-decree corpora, and new litigation datasets beyond DAIL/Charlotin — and every candidate is a logged object, a web-app with no open data, a commercial dashboard, or a journal-article analysis.

The one genuinely fresh object has no usable data layer. "AI on Trial" (Isadora Valadares Assunção, USP/Techlab) is a cross-national coded AI-litigation project — 500 cases across 39 countries/jurisdictions, manually coded by jurisdiction, court/administrative authority, AI-issue (bias/transparency/IP), and year, covering judicial and administrative rulings (Tech Policy Press). Its jurisdiction breadth exceeds the US-centric DAIL (~260) and Algorithmic Contestability DB (168). But access is a Streamlit dashboard only (aiontrial.streamlit.app) — no bulk download, CSV, API, repository, or license, and the write-up reports only aggregate counts (legal-profession 92, IP 56, administrative-use 49). It is one researcher's curated project (assembled to be studied), dates to May 2025, and sits in the heavily-logged AI-litigation genre — web-app-only, like the rejected commercial trackers. ~14/25.

Everything else repeated known kills. "Visible to the Court" (559 federal opinions, GovInfo) is the rejected anonymized-paper artifact (10-28, 11-17) (arXiv 2607.23888). The Mdpi study (347 AI US cases, ML text analytics) is a journal article with no released case set (MDPI). The J.S. Held AI Disputes Monitor (launched March 2026) is a commercial dashboard over third-party sources. gemmozero/ai-lawsuits-2026 and emperor-mew/federal-courts on HF are automated news-scrape collections (the vendor-feed reject genre). The Princeton "LLM-as-Judge" machine-coded Courts of Appeals DB uses AI to code appeals cases — AI is the tool, not the subject — so it has no AI-litigation nexus (off-object). No amicus-brief or consent-decree AI corpus surfaced.

The Charlotin AI Discovery DB watch (10-28) is unchanged. Same posture; flagging, not editing the rotation.
