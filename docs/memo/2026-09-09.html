The AI industry's election war chest

FEC filings for "Leading the Future," the AI industry's super PAC — who funds it, how much it's raised, and which 2026 midterm candidates the money is backing.

September 9, 2026
22/25
Willis score

Third pass on frontier 2 (08-10 FARA/electoral bonds; 08-20 USASpending AI procurement, AI lobbying). Those covered AI procurement and lobbying; the un-mined money trail is campaign spending — the AI industry's brand-new electoral machine.

Top find: Leading the Future — the AI industry's super PAC (FEC filings)

"Leading the Future" (FEC committee C00916114), launched August 2025 by a16z and OpenAI figures to elect pro-AI candidates and punish AI-regulation supporters, is the AI Fairshake — and its FEC filings are open, itemized, and updated every reporting period. Two watchdog trackers already sit on it (Demand Progress's AI Money Watch, Transformer News), but there is no academic panel of AI-industry campaign finance.

AI nexus + agenda. Bullseye Representation (and Governance): the AI industry's money entering elections to shape who writes AI law. The AI nexus is the dataset — this is the industry's political arm.

Why it clears the bar. Multimillion-dollar gifts and independent expenditures under electoral stakes — revealed behavior, not stated preference (a5); FEC filing exhaust that accretes every period (b4); academically unclaimed — journalists and watchdogs track it, but a Scholar check finds no scholarly panel (c4); panel-able by donor × committee × date and committee × candidate × race (d4); dead-on agenda (e5). Score: 22/25 (a5 b4 c4 d4 e5).

Evidence from a live sample. I queried the FEC API for C00916114 this run: 2026-cycle receipts $75.79M, disbursements $44.76M, cash-on-hand $31.03M (through 2026-06-30). The stunner is concentration — only 20 itemized receipts fund the whole thing: A16Z Capital Management gave $25M; Ben Horowitz, Marc Andreessen, OpenAI president Greg Brockman, and Anna Brockman gave $12.5M each; Perplexity chipped in $100K. A handful of a16z- and OpenAI-linked donors are the war chest. Artifacts in analysis/2026-09-09/. Per reporting, the wider LTF network (with Think Big PAC and American Mission PAC) has raised >$125M (CNBC, NOTUS).

Access & cost. Free FEC API (a personal key lifts the DEMO_KEY 40/hr cap); Schedule A/B + independent-expenditure endpoints give donors, disbursements, and race-level spend. A weekend builds the donor→PAC→candidate graph. Nearest work / difference: the trackers total the money; nobody has modeled it as a panel — donor concentration, which races the spend targets, and whether it moves AI-policy roll-call votes. Headline figure: LTF independent expenditures by race and party over the 2026 cycle, against each targeted member's AI-regulation position.

Runner-up: Data-center subsidy tracker (Good Jobs First)

The other AI money trail is the public subsidy of the AI compute buildout. Good Jobs First's Subsidy Tracker records (per Newsweek) 251 data-center subsidy awards across 16 states, 2020–2026 — Washington 120+, Texas ~86 — with packages up to $8.2B (Amazon, Indiana), $1B (Amazon, Oregon), $891M (Apple, NC), $687.6M (Meta, TX). AI nexus: the hyperscale AI buildout financed by state tax breaks; money trail + governance/transparency (only ~11 states disclose recipients). Honest caveat: the tracker covers data centers broadly, not AI-only, and I could not fetch the tracker itself this run (Good Jobs First returned 403), so figures are via Newsweek. Score: 19/25 (a4 b4 c3 d4 e4).
