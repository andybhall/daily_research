# 2026-10-05 — AI & algorithmic-management clauses in collective bargaining agreements

**Object:** The WageIndicator Collective Agreements Database and its AI / algorithmic-management
clause layer (built with UNI Europa + Friedrich-Ebert-Stiftung) — a coded, cross-national record
of what organized labor actually bargains for when it confronts workplace AI.

## Verified this run (all from pages fetched 2026-10-05)
- WageIndicator CBA database: **3,600+ CBAs across 76 countries**, collected/annotated since 2011
  (uni-europa.org). Dashboard maps AI & algorithmic-management clauses across Europe, "updated yearly."
- Clause categories coded: data protection, monitoring/surveillance, algorithmic management,
  human oversight of AI, information/consultation, training/digital skills, cybersecurity
  (wageindicator.org).
- UNI Europa 2024 survey of **148 affiliates across 32 countries**: **20%** already have CBAs
  addressing AI; **42%** are currently negotiating AI-related issues (uni-europa.org).
- Archival home: WageIndicator Collective Agreements Database is catalogued in CESSDA.

## Why it matters / agenda
Representation (organized worker voice bargaining over AI) + Governance (private, binding
governance of workplace AI). Intrinsic AI nexus. This is the *deposited, international, coded*
counterpart to the 08-26 US "build-it-yourself" union-AI-clause find (NewsGuild/WGA/SAG + NLRB).

## Honest caveats
- The underlying CBA texts are publicly browsable and the broader DB is archived in CESSDA, but a
  clean machine-readable AI-clause CSV may require the CESSDA deposit or a WageIndicator data request;
  the AI-clause coding is primarily surfaced via the dashboard + downloadable UNI Europa/FES PDFs.
- The detailed per-clause-type prevalence lives in image-based PDFs I could not parse this run; the
  chart uses the authoritative UNI Europa survey headline (have vs negotiating).
- Adjacent to (not a re-log of) the 08-26 US union-AI find: different geography (76 countries),
  and an actually-deposited coded dataset rather than an assemble-it-yourself corpus.
